Scope and verdict

I concentrated on the finite E6/E7/E8 assertion, its computational proof, and priority. I did not referee the rank-uniform theorem in full. I find no mathematical obstruction in the finite argument. Correctness confidence is high. Historical-novelty confidence is moderate, not high: I found no direct predecessor proving the exact arbitrary-upper, FC-lower E theorem, but neither a web search nor the supplied previous audits can certify its absence.

The finite theorem is potentially publishable as a concise completion of the finite simply laced case, with an explicit, reproducible exceptional terminal classification. It is not a major new method for KL polynomials. E6/E7 computations alone would be a weak submission today; including E8 and identifying precisely how little computation is necessary substantially improves the case. A strong specialist journal may still judge this incremental. I would not recommend rejection simply because the exceptional cases are finite or because the proof uses computation. Conversely, successful verification does not by itself establish novelty or journal-level interest.

What is new mathematical content, if priority survives

The valuable finite output is an exhaustive classification of the obstructions to the familiar star reduction: 1, 4, and 6 noncommuting terminals, including an E8 full-support terminal of length 50. This is more useful than a giant list of mu values, but remains a finite classification. The parabolic-pruning lemma is an immediate consequence of length-additive parabolic factorization; do not advertise that lemma alone as a substantial new structural theorem. The practical implementation is excellent for making the result checkable.

There are no new difficult exceptional polynomial evaluations: all but one terminal pattern vanish by parity, and the remaining pattern is Gern's D6 pair. This sharply limits claims about new coefficient-computation techniques. It also makes the theorem elegant: the real exceptional content is exhaustiveness, not brute-force KL recursion.

A stronger, natural corollary should be explicit: for every finite simply laced Coxeter group W, FC x and arbitrary w imply mu(x,w) in {0,1}. Green handles A, Gern handles D, and the submitted theorem handles E. Reducible groups follow from P_{x,w}=product_i P_{x_i,w_i}. If k>=2 factors have positive gaps d_i, then deg P <= sum_i (d_i-1)/2=(d-k)/2<(d-1)/2, so mu=0; if just one factor changes, use that factor's theorem. FC is componentwise. This is a genuine completion in scope, though the corollary's deduction is elementary. It cannot honestly be advertised as a known longstanding open problem without stronger historical evidence.

Adding Chmutov's B/C result does NOT establish all finite crystallographic types: F4 still needs an appropriate theorem or a fresh certified check. G2 is dihedral and elementary. Neither Jones's upper-Deodhar theorem nor Green's both-endpoints-FC theorem supplies the missing F4 hypothesis. Do not state an all-finite-crystallographic conclusion on the references presently cited.

Correctness and computational audit

The star reduction is sound. After removing covers and even gaps, descent containment puts both endpoints in the appropriate star domain. FC preservation and symmetric star transport apply. The upper length decreases and the gap remains positive (d or d-2), avoiding the orientation problem at gap one. Commuting terminals are elementary.

E8 exhaustiveness is not a bounded search disguised as a proof. Unique parabolic factorization, hereditary right terminality of its parabolic factor, complete coset closure, and filtering by the exact root-sum test prove completeness. Inverse filtering then gives the two-sided classification. I read recursive_terminals.cpp and the underlying root arithmetic in parabolic_terminals.cpp, as well as the independent verifier and reports. The byte-index representation accommodates all 240 E8 roots and eight simple-root images. Full equality, rather than only a hash, identifies states.

It terminated successfully, reproducing 143,660 tested products over all stages, 2,160 right terminals, 58 commuting terminals, six bad terminals, and 10,846 FC elements. I did not rerun the entire D7 engine or the full E6/E7 enumerations during this review. Their reports and certificates are supporting evidence, not fresh independent runs by me.

The D6 appeal is properly attributed and legitimate. For the 0/1 theorem alone Gern's type-D theorem suffices; Lemma 4.5.5 supplies the exact value 1 claimed for the residual row. His original numerical lemma used Coxeter software. The present manuscript should retain its computer-assisted description and explain that the D6 input is old. A separately supplied recurrence certificate is useful supplementary verification, not new mathematical priority.

An important simplification is currently missed: the manuscript's maximum-common-commuting-descents lemma already proves uniqueness of every finite eligible bottom. For each row, I=L(b)=R(b) is maximum independent on supp(b). The six E8 support types are D4, D4 x A1, D4 x A1, D6, E7, E8; their independence numbers are 3,4,4,4,4,4. I checked these from the reproduced words by exhaustive independent-set enumeration; the E6/E7 rows are the corresponding subdiagram rows. Tiny matching bounds also prove the numbers by hand. Applying the lemma forces x=i(I), and Bruhat inclusion follows because I is a commuting descent prefix. Thus FC catalogues can be supplementary checks; the logical finite proof needs only the terminal classification plus the structural lemma. This would unify the finite and infinite portions and improve the paper substantially.
