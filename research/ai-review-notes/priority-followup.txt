2. A rigorous reason cell W-graphs alone do not settle the residual pairs

Geck–Halls, On the Kazhdan–Lusztig cells in type E8, Math. Comp. 84 (2015), 3029–3049, DOI https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/2963; preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.6804. Local full text: research/exceptional_priority/geck-halls.txt. Remark 2.1, printed preprint p.4, gives the W-graph action: T_s e_y is scalar when s is a left descent of y; otherwise its off-diagonal terms are tilde-mu(x,y)e_x for x with s a left descent. Consequently a pair is visible in this action only across a difference of left descent sets. If L(x)=L(y), neither orientation contributes an off-diagonal action entry. The right action has the analogous restriction using R. This is an elementary consequence of the displayed multiplication formula, not an inference from missing keywords.

The actual PyCox wgraph constructor (9783–9808) applies the condition “s in self.Isets[x] and not s in self.Isets[y]”. The terminal residual pairs in this manuscript have equal left and right descent sets. Thus even complete action matrices would not directly display these residual values. This does NOT mean that intermediate polynomial caches never compute them, that information cannot be recovered by additional calculations, or that the general KL algorithm lacks the capability.

Suggested manuscript wording:
“The exceptional cell computations of Geck–Halls do not by themselves provide the residual coefficients used below: the W-graph action records a pair only across a difference of descent sets [Remark 2.1], whereas our residual pairs have equal left and right descent sets.”
A footnote may add that this concerns published outputs, not possible intermediate calculations.

Geck–Halls Sections 4 and 6 concern leading coefficients of character values, distinguished involutions, a-values, and reconstruction from 106 cells. Those representation-theoretic leading coefficients must not be identified with the ordinary KL polynomial coefficient mu(x,w) appearing here. The Geck PyCox paper, https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.5566, supplies prior general algorithms rather than the specific theorem under review. No FC-lower/all-upper 0/1 assertion or the manuscript's exact non-FC terminal lists was located in these primary texts or this official data interface.

3. Full Shi papers: hypotheses, conclusions, and relevance

Downloaded and text-extracted both papers from Nankai's primary institutional mirrors:
A. Left cells containing a fully commutative element:

B. Fully commutative elements and Kazhdan–Lusztig cells, II:
https://cfc.nankai.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/33/c9/99a45e764e18b406dc60b6f33ed6/8e272b77-5452-43ef-b007-c55b9d861091.pdf
Saved as priority-sources/shi-leftcells.pdf/.txt and shi-fccells-ii.pdf/.txt. These replaced failed direct ECNU links fulc3b.pdf and fulc1.pdf; failures must not be represented as absent papers.

Paper A is 14 pages. Its Section 1.8 defines Fc as a SUBSET OF FC ELEMENTS with a one-sided descent-removal property, and Fc' as a still smaller subset. Its Theorem 2.1 (p.7) describes elements z in the SAME LEFT CELL as w in Fc': z is a length-additive left extension of w; uniqueness follows under additional minimality conditions. Lemma 1.9 gives a descending right-star sequence inside Fc' to w_K. This is relevant structural background, but is not a classification of arbitrary non-FC elements terminal on both sides. In particular its Fc notation should not be mistaken for the non-FC terminal obstruction set. The full proof and concluding remarks develop left-cell minimal elements, not numerical 0/1 bounds for ordinary mu with arbitrary upper endpoint. The Introduction explicitly says its Weyl/affine Weyl conclusion was already obtained in Shi's earlier Fully commutative elements in Weyl and affine Weyl groups; that earlier result should be respected as prior cell structure, not misreported as the bound here.

Paper B, Theorem 2.1 (p.5), equates FC closure under the indicated two-sided preorder with being a union of two-sided cells. Immediately preceding it, and again in Lemma 2.2's proof, the applicable irreducible diagrams are nonbranching, excluding affine F4. The branching D/E diagrams therefore cannot be brought into the proposed 0/1 theorem by this equivalence. Its Appendix gives certain low-rank right-cell graphs, not E6/E7/E8 terminal lists. Its graphs use nonvanishing KL adjacency, which alone does not specify the magnitude of every coefficient. No present bound or exact terminal lists were found in these two full primary texts.

Additional related source previously examined: Green–Losonczy https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0102003, Theorem 2.2.3 and Example 2.2.5. The projection/cell-closure hypotheses fail in D and E; one cannot simply invoke that theorem for exceptional finite ADE. The direct Shi checks support, rather than replace, this distinction.
