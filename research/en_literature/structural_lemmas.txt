STRUCTURAL LEMMAS FOR THE GENERALIZED E_n INVESTIGATION
Checked 2026-10-07 UTC. Graph convention: chain 0--1--...--(n-2),
with vertex n-1 attached to 2; equivalently arms of lengths 1,2,n-4.
Ordinary KL theory throughout uses equal parameters q=v^2.

These are deductions from the cited sources, not claims of new priority.

1. UNIQUENESS OF AN ELIGIBLE FC BOTTOM

Let W=W(E_n), n>=6. Let J be any subset of the simple generators, and let
I be an independent set of the induced graph on J of maximum cardinality.
Suppose x is fully commutative, supp(x) is contained in J, and
I is contained in L(x) intersect R(x). Then x=i(I), the product of I.

Proof. Write a_TL(x) for Green's monomial Temperley--Lieb a-function: it is
the greatest cardinality of an independent set A whose product i(A) occurs
as a length-additive factor of x. Because I is in L(x), the standard
parabolic decomposition gives x=i(I)y reduced. Consequently a_TL(x)>=|I|.
Every independent factor of x is supported in J, so a_TL(x)<=alpha(J)=|I|.
Green's Proposition 4.4(iv) now puts x in the monomial right cell of i(I).
The same argument applied on the right puts x in its monomial left cell.
Proposition 4.4(v) says this left/right cell intersection has one element.
It already contains i(I), proving the assertion.

The proof actually needs only a_TL(x)=|I|, together with both descent
inclusions. It does not identify the TL cells with ordinary KL cells.
Nor does it require I to be maximum in the whole ambient E_n graph:
maximum in J suffices, since supp(x) is contained in J.

Corollary. If b has L(b)=R(b)=I, with I maximum independent in supp(b),
then every FC x<=b satisfying L(b) subset L(x) and R(b) subset R(x)
equals i(I). This removes the eligible-bottom enumeration for such b.

Primary foundation: R.M. Green, On the Markov trace for Temperley--Lieb
algebras of type E_n, arXiv:0704.0283v1, Definition 4.1 (p.9),
Proposition 4.4(iv),(v) (pp.10--11). The last paragraph of the proof of
Lemma 5.2 (pp.13--14) uses essentially this same cell-intersection argument.
https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0283
Local: green-en-trace.pdf and green-en-trace.txt.

Elementary auxiliary computation: alpha(E_n)=ceil(n/2). If the trivalent
vertex is excluded, the three residual paths give
1+1+ceil((n-4)/2)=ceil(n/2). If it is included, deleting its neighbours
leaves one vertex on the arm of length2 and a path of length n-5,
giving 1+1+ceil((n-5)/2)=floor(n/2). Thus the maximum is as stated.

For the two investigated full-support E9 terminals of lengths32 and34,
I={1,3,5,7,8} has size5=alpha(E9). Their only eligible FC bottom is
therefore 13578, provided the stated descent sets and Bruhat eligibility
are checked. Those checks are in the independent computational records.

2. DEGREE VANISHING FROM AN ORDINARY a-LOWER BOUND

Let (W,S) be ANY Coxeter system with equal parameters. Let I be a commuting
set, x=i(I)<b, and assume I subset L(b) (a right-hand version is identical).
Then

  P_(x,b)=P_(e,b),
  deg P_(x,b) <= (ell(b)-a(b))/2.

The first identity follows by successively applying P_(y,b)=P_(sy,b)
when s belongs to L(b), deleting the letters of the commuting product x.
The second follows from Lusztig's P1:

  a(b)<=Delta(b)=ell(b)-2 deg P_(e,b).

Thus if a(b)>|I|+1, the coefficient at degree
(ell(b)-|I|-1)/2 vanishes: mu(x,b)=0. When the gap is even, mu is zero
already by parity. The strict inequality is essential.

Source scope: Lusztig, Hecke algebras with unequal parameters, REVISED
2014 arXiv:math/0208154v2, section15.1 (printed p.63) explicitly notes that
P1--P6 need no boundedness assumption in the equal-parameter case.
Section15.2 proves P1 using positivity. Section14.1 defines Delta.
The original 2003 edition does not incorporate Elias--Williamson positivity;
cite the revised version when using the all-Coxeter scope.
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208154v2
Local: lusztig-unequal.pdf and lusztig-unequal.txt.

3. A FINITE PARABOLIC FACTOR IS A SUFFICIENT WITNESS

In the setting of Lemma2, suppose b=u w_K v is length additive, W_K is
finite, and w_K is its longest element. If ell(w_K)>=|I|+2, then mu(x,b)=0.

Proof. Repeated KL multiplication by simple generators along the reduced
factorization gives b <=_LR w_K. P4 therefore gives a(b)>=a(w_K).
The parabolic Hecke subalgebra has the same KL basis and multiplication
coefficients on W_K as the ambient algebra. In it,

  C_(w_K) C_(w_K) = (sum_(z in W_K) v^(2ell(z)-ell(w_K))) C_(w_K).

The scalar has degree ell(w_K), whence directly a_W(w_K)>=ell(w_K).
Combining this with Lemma2 proves the claim. This argument needs only the
inequality from the subalgebra; it does NOT assume P12 (ambient/parabolic
equality of a-functions). This matters for indefinite E_n.

P4 is proved without boundedness by Lusztig section15.5 together with
section14.4, printed pp.64 and59. Invariance of a under ordinary star
operations also follows: stars relate elements in the same ordinary
one-sided cell, and P4 in both directions gives equality. Hence it is
enough to find such a longest factor in ANY star-equivalent element of b.

For I of size5 in E9, a longest factor of length7 or more suffices. The
family agent has found such a factor for the length32 terminal, of type
A1+A2+A2, J={0,2,3,5,6}. The exact factorization is saved separately in
research/en_families/parabolic_factor_search.json. At the time of this
note, its direct factor search found no length>=7 witness in the two
length34 reflection extensions. Absence of such a factor does not imply
a(b)<=6 and does not determine their mu values.

4. THE FC a-FUNCTION IS KNOWN IN ALL GENERALIZED E_n

Green--Xu prove ordinary a(x)=n(x), the width of the FC heap, in every
star-reducible Coxeter group. Their proof reduces x by stars to a commuting
product and observes that each step preserves heap width. All generalized
E_n are star reducible. Hence on FC elements ordinary a equals a_TL for
every n>=6, including n>=10; this is not limited to finite/affine Weyl groups.
It gives no value for a non-FC terminal upper endpoint.

Primary: R.M. Green and T. Xu, Classification of Coxeter groups with finitely
many elements of a-value2, Algebraic Combinatorics3(2020), 331--364,
Proposition3.15, published p.343. Proposition3.13 cites Shi's earlier
finite/affine result. https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/alco.95.pdf
Local: green-xu-a2-classification.pdf and .txt.

Star reducibility: R.M. Green, Star reducible Coxeter groups,
Glasgow Math. J.48(2006), 583--609, arXiv:math/0509363v2,
Theorem5.11 and Theorem6.3. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0509363
Local: green-star-reducible.pdf and .txt.

5. TL PROJECTION: USEFUL FINITE DATA, BUT NOT A PROOF OF THE BOUND

For a star-reducible group the quotient theta:H-->TL sends every ordinary
KL basis element C'_w into the canonical TL lattice. Bar invariance implies
theta(C'_w) is an integer linear combination of canonical TL basis elements.
For E_n the latter agree with the monomial basis. Thus finite FC support
gives a finite-dimensional receptacle for all these images even if W is
infinite. However theta(C'_w)=0 is established by the weakly-complex lemma
only when w=sx is non-FC and x is FC. One must not replace this hypothesis
with arbitrary non-FC w.

Primary locators: Green, Star reducible Coxeter groups, Theorem4.3,
Lemma4.5, Theorem4.6; Theorem1.4(iv) for integral bar-fixed expansion.
Its Theorem4.1 says an arbitrary element is a commuting product or has a
noncommuting initial/final pair or a fork-shaped initial triple. This is
useful reduction structure, not a classification of all non-FC terminals.

Likewise the trace mu formula in Green's E_n Markov-trace paper,
Theorem1.2 and Corollary9.3, assumes BOTH endpoints FC. It cannot be
applied to arbitrary terminal upper endpoints.
