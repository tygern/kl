## Assessment

Subject to correctness of the computational classification and symbolic proofs, this is a credible, publishable specialist contribution. The finite theorem appears to be a new completion of the fully commutative lower-endpoint 0–1 problem in finite simply laced type. Its mathematically decisive new input is the complete terminal classification in (E_6,E_7,E_8), particularly the two exceptional seeds of lengths 28 and 50. The rank-uniform full-support terminal construction is a worthwhile second result. I found no primary source stating the claimed exceptional bound or the same exceptional terminal lists or infinite (E)-families.

This is a positive but bounded novelty judgment, not a certification of priority. The work applies an existing reduction strategy and existing Temperley–Lieb cell theory to new exceptional data. I would describe it as a strong specialized completion and construction paper, not a new general theory of Kazhdan–Lusztig coefficients. Nothing in the inspected literature makes the main theorem an immediate corollary *without* establishing the exceptional terminal classification or an equivalent new exceptional input.

## What is new, and what is inherited

1. **Finite theorem and classification.** After accepting the terminal table, the finite theorem follows quickly from established star transport, Fan's cell facts, parity, and one type-(D_6) coefficient already in Gern. This is not a reason to discount the theorem: the table supplies precisely the information that those earlier results do not supply. The claim should continue to identify the exhaustive exceptional classification as the new finite ingredient. The current introduction does this unusually clearly.

2. **Maximum common descents.** The manuscript's lemma is a direct corollary of Fan's monomial cell theory, with essentially the same singleton-intersection maneuver already in Green's proof of Lemma 5.2. Its formulation for a maximum independent set on the support is useful packaging, but not a major independent theorem. The present citations and explicit attribution correctly acknowledge this.

3. **Parabolic pruning.** The completeness argument is mathematically sound at the conceptual level: a right-terminal element has a right-terminal standard parabolic factor. The algorithm is a useful implementation of a simple observation, not a deep new general enumeration method. Its value here is turning the (E_8) claim into a compact, reproducible computation instead of a huge table of KL polynomials.

4. **Infinite constructions.** The substantive content is finding explicit real roots or involutions, proving their full support and terminality for every parameter, and obtaining a family uniform in rank. In particular, the identity proving the seed is real, \(\beta_r=s_0F\alpha_0\), is more than a numerical norm check. The affine-translation iteration is standard reflection geometry once suitable seeds have been found, but the simultaneous descent and edge inequalities are specific mathematical constructions.

5. **Vanishing for the constructed families.** Once their maximum common descents and parity are proved, all non-cover coefficients vanish by the inherited lemma. Since every fixed (E_n) is FC-finite, only finitely many short upper endpoints in a pairwise distinct family can possibly have FC covers. Thus eventual cover exclusion is automatic at fixed rank. The manuscript's reflection-cover lemma has the useful additional content of removing those finite exceptions uniformly in rank, without separate FC catalogues. The affine construction uses a finite base-cover check. FC-finiteness by itself does **not** show arbitrary long non-cover coefficients vanish: equal-descent odd-gap pairs remain unless additional structure is used.

6. **Scope of the infinite result.** Infinitely many terminals in every (E_n), (n\geq9), already follow from one affine-(E_8) family and standard parabolic embeddings. The (E_{10}) and (E_{4r+1}) constructions add full support in those ambient ranks. They do not classify all terminals, establish a 0–1 theorem for arbitrary upper endpoints in affine (E_8), or give full-support families in every rank. The manuscript does not currently claim these stronger statements.

## Priority and presentation cautions

- Preserve the qualification “to our knowledge.” Searches for the exceptional coefficient bound, bad/weakly bad/T-avoiding elements, star-irreducible elements, and later citing literature found no competing theorem, but cannot exclude unpublished computations, an unindexed thesis, or an implicit observation elsewhere.
- The negative universal example in affine (D_4) should not be marketed as a new refutation of a still-open universal conjecture. It is already derivable from [Mongelli's boolean-element formulas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.2945v1), Corollaries 2 and 3. The manuscript credits this, and uses the example as a scope limitation, appropriately.
- “Completes the finite simply laced case” is a defensible and useful conclusion. “Solves Green's conjecture” without that scope would misstate the result.
- Retain the distinction between ordinary KL polynomials, quotient monomial cells, and parabolic KL polynomials. The manuscript's discussions presently make this distinction correctly.
- No concrete missing citation discovered here undermines the main proof or priority claim. The two improvements justified by the search are optional: explicitly mention Laird's finite-(E_n) open-problem sentence, and optionally acknowledge the 2025 affine-(B,D) FC irreducibility paper.

## Referee recommendation on significance

I would support publication in a research journal serving algebraic combinatorics, Coxeter groups, or computational algebra, assuming the independent proof/code review confirms correctness and a durable, self-contained computational supplement is deposited. The complete finite ADE conclusion is a clean answer to a natural question. The tiny exceptional terminal list and the fact that its new residual cases vanish by parity make the result particularly intelligible.

The contribution is not diminished merely because the last inference is short; the relevant question is whether the classification is new, exhaustive, and reproducible. Conversely, the large order of (W(E_8)) should not be used alone as a measure of conceptual depth. The most accurate significance statement combines a finite exceptional completion, a transparent computational classification, and explicit full-support constructions in infinite type. The present manuscript largely achieves that calibration.

The outstanding uncertainty from this subreview is historical priority in inaccessible or unpublished sources, especially Graham's original thesis; it is not a discovered contrary theorem. Mathematical and computational correctness require the separate verification underway.
