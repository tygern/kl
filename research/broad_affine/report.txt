AFFINE GEOMETRY / UNFOLDING RESEARCH REPORT
Date: 2026-10-07 UTC. Scope: a focused search, not a novelty certification.

RESULT
No substantive new affine family with a proved finite-D FC-bottom interval
witness was found. Two precise obstructions were obtained instead: (A) the
unbounded Gern bad-interval family cannot be realized in any FIXED affine Weyl
group; (B) even the elementary principal interval below the square of a
bipartite Coxeter element in affine D4 has no PRINCIPAL finite-D interval model.
Neither obstruction excludes finite-D models with arbitrary FC nonidentity
bottoms, nor other unbounded source families, nor affine groups of growing rank.

A. COATOM OBSTRUCTION TO FIXED-AFFINE-RANK REALIZATIONS OF GERN I_n

Primary source verified directly:
Grant T. Barkley and Christian Gaetz, Bounded Bruhat intervals and affine
Coxeter groups, arXiv:2606.16894v1, Proposition 2.1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16894v1#S2.SS1
The displayed bound is |U(w)|,|D(w)| <= |Phi_fin|. Phi_fin means ALL finite
roots, not positive roots: their proof explicitly ends with
2|Phi_fin^+| = |Phi_fin|. Consequently every interval in affine D_r has at most
2r(r-1) coatoms, irrespective of endpoint length or lower FC status.
The proof uses extreme rays of Dyer's cover-root cone and at most two affine
roots from each family parallel to a finite positive root.

Exact computations:
gern_coatoms.py tests all n(n-1) signed-transposition reflections in D_n,
using the signed inversion length formula and an independent lifting-property
Bruhat comparison (existing computations/sparse_kl.py, read without editing).
The complete list of cover reflections, not merely the total, is in
research/broad_affine/gern_coatoms.json.
For n=4,6,...,24 the coatom counts of I_n are
6,12,20,30,42,56,72,90,110,132,156.
Every lower cover of w_n lies above x_n for these n.
The observed formula is n(n+2)/4. The report does NOT claim a proof of that
exact formula for all n.

Concrete certified consequence: I_10 has 30 coatoms, whereas affine D4 allows
at most 24. Thus I_10 cannot be poset-isomorphic to ANY affine-D4 interval.
Its rank is 34. The odd-rank example I_14 (rank 69) has 56 coatoms, and hence
also cannot occur in affine D4. These are exclusions independent of CI and KL.

A general elementary proof that the coatom counts are unbounded:
Use the existing signed convention: s0 negates/swaps positions 1,2;
s_i swaps i,i+1 for 1<=i<n. For even n>=4,
w_n(1)=(-1)^(n/2), w_n(i)=i for odd i>1,
w_n(i)=-(n+2-i) for even i.
Let x_n=s0 s1 s3 s5 ... s_(n-1), a product of commuting simple reflections.
For each even j in {2,4,...,n}, let t_(1,j) exchange positions 1 and j
WITHOUT changing signs, and set z_j=w_n t_(1,j).

1. z_j is a lower cover of w_n. The D length is
   inv(w)+#{i<k : w(i)+w(k)<0}.
   The second summand is unchanged by any ordinary permutation of positions.
   Write a=w_n(1) in {1,-1}, b=w_n(j)<0. We have b<a. Each intermediate
   entry w_n(k), 1<k<j, is either positive >=3>a (odd k), or is negative
   strictly below b (even k). Thus there are no intermediate entries strictly
   between b and a. Swapping entries at 1,j decreases inv by exactly one.

2. z_j has full simple support. For each 2<=k<n the first k absolute values
   cannot be {1,...,k}: if j>k they omit 1; if j<=k they include n (the value
   originally at position 2 stays in the initial block, including when j=2).
   Therefore z_j does not belong to the parabolic omitting s_k.
   It has a negative entry outside position 1 whose absolute value exceeds 1:
   at least one of the other n/2-1 even positions retains its original negative
   value. Hence it lies neither in the parabolic omitting s0 (all values
   positive) nor in the parabolic omitting s1. The latter is conjugate to the
   ordinary symmetric group by changing the sign of coordinate 1; its negative
   entries can occur only at position 1 or have absolute value 1.

3. A reduced expression for z_j therefore contains every generator of x_n.
   Select one occurrence of each of these mutually commuting generators.
   The selected subword has product x_n, so x_n<=z_j by the subword criterion.

The distinct z_j give at least n/2 coatoms of I_n. Thus if I_n occurs in
fixed affine D_r, necessarily n/2 <= 2r(r-1), or n<=4r(r-1).
More generally n<=2|Phi_fin| for any fixed affine Weyl group.
This deliberately uses the fully proved weaker n/2 bound; the stronger exact
quadratic formula remains a computational observation in this report.

B. DEGREE-FOUR OBSTRUCTION TO NAIVE PRINCIPAL-INTERVAL UNFOLDING

In affine D4 label the central simple reflection 2 and four commuting leaves
0,1,3,4. Let c=s0 s1 s3 s4 s2, and w=c^2. Then w has reduced length 10.
The faithful affine signed-permutation computation check_unfolding.py verifies
length 10 and enumerates [e,w] by deletion from reduced words. It has 448
vertices and rank vector
[1,5,14,32,63,98,109,80,36,9,1].
The JSON certificate is unfolding_obstruction.json.

Intrinsic poset construction: on the atoms of a principal interval [e,v],
join two atoms a,b when there exists a rank-three element having exactly
those two atoms below it. In a simply-laced Coxeter system, such an element
can only be sts=tst for adjacent simple generators s,t. Thus the graph
constructed from [e,v] is a subgraph of its simple Coxeter diagram.
For the present w, each of the four words s_i s2 s_i is a subword of c^2,
so the intrinsically recovered graph is exactly the four-leaf star.
Every finite-D diagram has maximum degree three, including disconnected
subgraphs. Therefore [e,c^2] is not isomorphic to [e,v] in ANY finite D_N.
This is an actual rank-ten obstruction, stronger than merely noting that the
affine Coxeter diagram does not embed into a finite-D diagram.
It DOES NOT rule out [a,b] in finite D_N with a nonidentity FC bottom.
Also it does not conflict with boolean Coxeter-element intervals: those have
no rank-three element on two atoms, and the above graph is empty.

Periodic heaps alone cannot bypass this issue: heap order parametrizes weak
order phenomena for FC elements, whereas strong Bruhat intervals identify
subexpressions through braid relations. The degree-four obstruction records
precisely such rank-three braid information.

C. RANK-THREE SEARCH (NEGATIVE RESULT, NOT A THEOREM)

I tested all affine-D4 elements through length20: 32,257 elements in the ball.
All rank-three intervals among these have 2,3,or4 coatoms (crown sizes).
Counts: 119,228 with 2; 2,928,558 with 3; 135,038 with 4.
Thus no easy oversized rank-three crown distinguishes these intervals from
finite simply-laced type. This is finite computational evidence only.
The model is a faithful action x -> p*x+t, p an even signed permutation,
t in the D4 coroot lattice. The affine generator flips/swaps the last two
coordinates and adds e3+e4. BFS gives exact reduced lengths; lower covers
are obtained by deletion and tested for length drop one. check_unfolding.py
also checks the affine-D4 Coxeter relations in this action.
Files: affine_d_crowns.py; crowns_D4_L10.json; crowns_D4_L20.json.

D. PRIMARY-SOURCE AUDIT OF CANDIDATE GEOMETRIC TRANSPORTS

1. Gaston Burrull, Nicolas Libedinsky, Rodrigo Villegas,
   Shape and class of Bruhat Intervals, arXiv:2507.14033v1.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14033v1
   Theorem C: classification of thick dominant affine-A2 intervals by polygon
   isometries. Sections4 and7 describe genuine piecewise translation maps.
   Proposition7.7 already proves KL invariance for those thick intervals.
   Conjecture7.8 is eventual poset stabilization under dominant translation
   in general affine Weyl groups; Proposition7.10 proves the affine-A2 case.
   General affine-D stabilization may not be asserted from this paper.
   Even stabilization by itself produces another AFFINE realization, not a
   finite-D witness. For finite-D witness work it leaves the central gap open.

2. Nathan Reading and Debra Waugh, The order dimension of Bruhat order on
   infinite Coxeter groups, Electronic J. Combin.11(2), R13 (2005), Prop9.1.
   https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v11i2r13/pdf/
   Their copies of finite W inside a minuscule affine quotient look promising
   geometrically, but the proof explicitly identifies the copy as the coset
   xW. Hence they are exactly within the excluded classical coset mechanism.

3. Barkley--Gaetz, arXiv:2606.16894v1, Theorem1.1 and proof.
   Finitely many isomorphism types in any fixed affine interval rank do NOT
   imply that every such interval has a finite Coxeter realization. The paper
   specifically observes that maximal-chain reflection subgroups can remain
   infinite affine groups; that is the obstacle it overcomes by coatom bounds.
   The theorem is useful here for the rigorous obstruction A, not a witness.

4. Direct folding is not an interval isomorphism. For example fold the three
   outer nodes a,b,c of finite D4 by triality: the folded generator maps to
   abc. In folded G2 the principal interval below st is a rank2 diamond with
   four vertices. Its natural unfolded image top abc*d is a Coxeter element
   in D4, whose principal interval is Boolean of rank4 with 16 vertices.
   Fixed-point descriptions therefore cannot be substituted for full ambient
   interval isomorphisms. This is an elementary obstruction to a tempting
   folding proof, not a theorem forbidding alternative interval models.
