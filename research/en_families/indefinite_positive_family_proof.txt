EXPLICIT INFINITE TERMINAL-REFLECTION FAMILIES IN INDEFINITE E10 AND E13
Research proof, 2026-10-07. Historical priority remains UNCONFIRMED.

NUMBERING AND RESULT

The E_n diagram is the chain 0--1--...--(n-2), with vertex n-1 attached
to 2. Roots use this simple-root coordinate order, squared lengths 2,
and inner products -1 on edges. All KL parameters are equal.

For each of the following data sets define

  beta_k = beta - a*k*gamma + (a*k^2+3*k)*delta,  k>=0,
  b_k = r_(beta_k),    x = product of the commuting generators in I.

E10:
  a=1,
  beta=(3,7,10,9,7,6,4,3,1,6),
  delta=(2,4,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,3),
  gamma=(1,2,3,2,2,1,1,0,0,1),
  I={1,3,5,7,9}.

E13:
  a=2,
  beta=(2,3,5,5,4,4,3,3,2,2,1,1,3),
  delta=(2,4,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,0,0,0,3),
  gamma=(1,2,3,2,2,1,1,0,0,0,0,0,1),
  I={0,3,5,7,9,11,12}.

THEOREM. Each sequence (b_k) consists of distinct, full-support,
non-fully-commutative terminal elements of the indicated fixed indefinite
Coxeter group. Both descent sets equal I. For every fully commutative y,
the ordinary leading coefficient mu(y,b_k) belongs to {0,1}; it vanishes
whenever the length gap is greater than one. The only FC y whose two
descent sets contain those of b_k is x, and mu(x,b_k)=0.

This is an explicit family theorem, not a classification of terminals or
of arbitrary upper endpoints in these groups. It uses no combinatorial
invariance theorem. Its structural FC input is a deduction from Green's
2007 monomial Temperley--Lieb cell results, cited below. No novelty claim
is made for either that input or this construction until priority is settled.

REAL ROOTS AND THE ALL-PARAMETER FORMULA

The nodes 0,...,7,n-1 form an affine E8 standard parabolic. Its null root
is delta. The vector gamma is a real root of its finite E8 subdiagram
obtained by omitting node7; gamma+delta is another real root. The finite
integer certificates in indefinite_positive_families.json give sequences
of simple reflections reducing beta, gamma, and gamma+delta to simple
roots. Thus all three are real roots of the ambient group. Explicit
reduction sequences for beta are recorded at the end of this proof.

Set T=r_gamma r_(gamma+delta). Since gamma^2=2, delta^2=0, and
(gamma,delta)=0, direct expansion gives, for EVERY ambient vector z,

  T(z)=z+(z,delta)gamma-((z,gamma)+(z,delta))delta.           (1)

In particular delta need not be radical in the ambient indefinite space.
The two seeds satisfy (beta,gamma)=-3 and (beta,delta)=-a. Equation (1),
together with T(delta)=delta and T(gamma)=gamma-2delta, implies

  T^k(beta)=beta-a*k*gamma+(a*k^2+3*k)delta=beta_k.         (2)

Therefore beta_k is real, has norm2, and b_k=T^k r_beta T^(-k).
An independent all-k certificate verifies T(beta)=beta+v, T(v)=v+u,
T(u)=u, where v=(a+3)delta-a gamma and u=2a delta; induction yields the
equivalent formula beta_k=beta+k*v+binom(k,2)*u. Both v and u are
coordinatewise nonnegative and beta is strictly positive. Thus beta_k
is strictly positive in every simple-root coordinate for every k>=0.

DESCENTS AND TERMINALITY

Write m_j(k)=(alpha_j,beta_k). Exact Cartan multiplication gives:

E10:
  m(k)=(-1,1,-2-k,1+k,-1-k,1+k,-1-k,1+k,
         -1-3*k-k^2,2+k).

E13:
  m(k)=(1,-1,-1-2*k,1+2*k,-1-2*k,1+2*k,-1-2*k,1+2*k,
         -1-3*k-2*k^2,1,-1,1,1+2*k).

In each case m_j(k)>0 exactly for j in I, while m_j(k)<=0 elsewhere.
Moreover m_s(k)+m_t(k)<=0 for every edge s--t with s in I. These are
explicit polynomial inequalities for k>=0, not numerical extrapolations.

For a strictly positive integral root beta_k,

  b_k(alpha_j)=alpha_j-m_j(k)beta_k.

This is a negative root exactly when m_j(k)>0. Consequently R(b_k)=I.
Since b_k is an involution, L(b_k)=I as well. If s in I and t is adjacent,

  b_k(alpha_s+alpha_t)=alpha_s+alpha_t
                         -(m_s(k)+m_t(k))*beta_k > 0.

But (b_k s)(alpha_t)=b_k(alpha_s+alpha_t), so after deleting any right
descent s, no adjacent generator t becomes a right descent. The same
holds on the left. This is precisely the terminal/weakly-bad condition:
no reduced word begins or ends with two noncommuting generators.

FULL SUPPORT, MAXIMAL DESCENTS, DISTINCTNESS

Every beta_k is positive in every coordinate. A proper standard
parabolic omitting node i preserves the alpha_i coordinate of every
vector. The reflection formula changes that coordinate on some alpha_j,
because beta_(k,i)>0 and some m_j(k) is nonzero. Hence b_k has full support.

The displayed I sets are independent and have maximum sizes5 and7.
A short upper-bound certificate is a matching: for E10 use edges
(0,1),(2,9),(3,4),(5,6),(7,8); for E13 use
(0,1),(2,12),(3,4),(5,6),(7,8),(9,10). An independent set uses at most
one endpoint of each matching edge and every unmatched vertex, giving
the respective upper bounds5 and7 attained by I.

The positive roots beta_k are distinct: v is nonzero and v,u>=0, so their
heights strictly increase. Equal reflections in nonzero norm2 roots
would imply roots differing only by sign. Therefore b_k are distinct,
and their lengths are unbounded because a finitely generated Coxeter
group has finite balls. No exact all-k length formula is needed here.

FC BOTTOM UNIQUENESS AND THE LEADING COEFFICIENT

Use the following maximum-independent lemma: in generalized E_n, if
an FC element y has a maximum independent set I contained in both
L(y) and R(y), then y=product I. To see this, Green's monomial TL
a-function is at least |I| from the initial commuting factor and at
most |I| by maximality. Proposition4.4(iv) puts y in the same monomial
right cell as product I, and the analogous argument gives the same
left cell. Proposition4.4(v) makes their intersection a singleton.
This is precisely the deduction proved in
research/en_literature/structural_lemmas.txt; it does not identify these
monomial cells with ordinary KL cells.

The commuting product x is a right parabolic factor of b_k and hence
lies below b_k. It has odd length |I|. Every reflection has odd length,
so ell(b_k)-ell(x) is even and mu(x,b_k)=0 by parity. If any other FC
y<b_k has gap>1, some upper left or right descent is absent from the
corresponding descent set of y. The ordinary KL descent criterion then
forces mu(y,b_k)=0. Covers have mu=1. This proves the asserted 0/1 bound.

The same uniqueness lemma shows the uppers are not FC: an FC b_k with
these descents would equal x. The latter is a product of at least five
distinct commuting reflections, whose geometric action has (-1)-eigenspace
of dimension |I|, whereas b_k has (-1)-eigenspace of dimension1.
