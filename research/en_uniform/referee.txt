INDEPENDENT REFEREE: RANK-UNIFORM GENERALIZED-E FAMILY
2026-10-07. Mathematical validity and historical priority are separate.

VERDICT

The two-parameter construction is valid for every integer r>=3 and k>=0.
It gives full-support, non-FC terminal reflections in E_(4r+1), with all
ordinary equal-parameter KL leading coefficients at FC lower endpoints
in {0,1}; every non-cover coefficient vanishes. For each fixed r, all of
these coefficients vanish for every sufficiently large k. The last claim
requires no length formula: FC-finiteness and injectivity of the sequence
suffice. No mathematical blocker was found in the final uniform section.

This is uniform over the unbounded rank class 13,17,21,... . It is not a
theorem for all upper endpoints, nor a full-support construction in every
congruence class of ranks, nor a formula for the complete polynomial P.
Priority of the explicit family is not settled by this correctness audit.

1. UNIFORM REAL-ROOT MEMBERSHIP

Use the chain 0--1--...--(4r-1), branch 4r attached to 2. Let N=4r. The
seed coefficients are b0=r-1, b1=r, b_j=2r-floor(j/2) for 2<=j<N, b_N=r.
Deleting node 0 gives finite D_N. In an orthonormal basis e1,...,eN, set
  alpha1=e1+e2,
  alpha_N=e2-e1,
  alpha_j=e_(j+1)-e_j  (2<=j<N).
Adjoin z orthogonal to the e-space, z^2=2-r, and put
  alpha0=z-(e1+...+eN)/2.
These N+1 linearly independent vectors have precisely the generalized-E
Cartan form. The negative value of z^2 causes no problem: this is the
indefinite ambient root space, and the D_N subspace remains Euclidean.

Expanding beta_r gives (r-1)z plus finite coordinates
  -(r-1)/2 on odd indices,  (3-r)/2 on even indices.
Since (beta_r,alpha0)=r-2, applying s0 gives coefficient one on z, finite
coordinates -1/2 on odd indices and +1/2 on even indices. Thus
  s0(beta_r)=F(alpha0),
where F changes the signs of precisely e2,e4,...,eN. This is an even
number 2r of sign changes, hence is in W(D_N). An explicit expression is
the product over j=1,...,r of
  r_(e_(4j-2)-e_(4j)) r_(e_(4j-2)+e_(4j)).
Each factor pair changes exactly those two coordinate signs; both roots
are D_N roots. Therefore beta_r=s0 F(alpha0) is a real root FOR EVERY r.
This step does not infer reality from norm two. For reference, cancellation
of the paired long-arm coordinates gives the norm identity
  (r-1)(r-2)+r(2-r)+r=2.

2. THE AMBIENT TRANSLATION, WITH ALL PARAMETERS

Let delta have coordinates (2,4,6,5,4,3,2,1) at 0,...,7 and 3 at branch N;
let gamma have coordinates (1,2,3,2,2,1,1,0) there and 1 at branch N.
All other coordinates are zero. The subdiagram on 0,...,7,N is affine E8.
Here delta^2=0, gamma^2=2, (gamma,delta)=0. Gamma and gamma+delta are real
roots; referee-certificate.json records explicit simple-reflection lowering
words, supported in this fixed subdiagram and independent of r after
renaming the branch. Both end at a simple root. Thus neither real-root
assertion rests solely on a norm test.

For T=r_gamma r_(gamma+delta), direct expansion gives, for EVERY ambient z,
  Tz=z+(z,delta)gamma-[(z,gamma)+(z,delta)]delta.
In particular delta is fixed, gamma maps to gamma-2delta, and Nop=T-1
satisfies Nop^3=0 on the whole ambient space. Delta is not an ambient
radical vector: (delta,alpha8)=-1. Treating it as radical would invalidate
the construction, but neither this proof nor the audited TeX does so.

Put a=2r-4. Direct Cartan pairings are
  (beta_r,gamma)=-r,  (beta_r,delta)=-a.
Consequently
  Nop beta_r=-a gamma+(a+r)delta=:v,
  Nop^2 beta_r=2a delta=:u,
  Nop u=0.
The binomial identity proves for every integer k>=0
  beta_(r,k)=T^k beta_r
            =beta_r-a k gamma+(a k^2+r k)delta
            =beta_r+k v+binom(k,2)u.
This both proves reality and fixes the sign/orientation of T. The first
increment at 0,...,7 and the branch is
  (4r-4,8r-8,12r-12,11r-12,8r-8,7r-8,4r-4,3r-4;7r-8).
It is positive there and zero elsewhere; u is nonnegative. Since the seed
is strictly positive, so is beta_(r,k) for every r>=3,k>=0.

3. ALL-PARAMETER DESCENTS AND TERMINALITY

The exact Cartan pairings m_j=(alpha_j,beta_(r,k)) are
  m0=r-2, m1=2-r, m2=-1-a k;
  m_j=(-1)^(j+1)(1+a k),  3<=j<=7;
  m8=-1-a k^2-r k;
  m_j=(-1)^(j+1),        9<=j<=4r-1;
  m_(4r)=1+a k.
Their positive set is exactly I_r={0,3,5,...,4r-1,4r}. Every m_j is nonzero.
On every edge incident to I_r the sum is zero except possibly 7--8 and
8--9. The exceptional sums respectively equal
  -r k-a k(k-1),   -(a+r)k-a k(k-1).
Both are nonpositive for integer k>=0. Writing the first this way makes
the k=0 and k=1 boundary cases explicit, including r>4.

For w=r_(beta_(r,k)), w(alpha_j)=alpha_j-m_j beta_(r,k). Strict positivity
and integrality of beta imply that this root is negative exactly on I_r.
Thus R(w)=I_r; involutivity gives L(w)=I_r. For s in I_r and t adjacent,
  w s(alpha_t)=w(alpha_t+alpha_s)
             =alpha_t+alpha_s-(m_t+m_s)beta_(r,k)
is positive. No adjacent generator descends after removal of a descent.
The same is true on the left. This proves the stipulated terminal property,
with no appeal to finite type or to a computer enumeration of group elements.

4. FULL SUPPORT, MAXIMUM INDEPENDENCE, NON-FC, DISTINCTNESS

Since m0=r-2>0 and every coordinate of beta is positive, column 0 of the
reflection differs from the identity in every coordinate row. Every simple
reflection except s_j leaves coordinate row j fixed. Hence an element of a
standard parabolic omitting j fixes that row; w belongs to no such parabolic
and has full support in the actual rank 4r+1.

I_r is independent of cardinality 2r+1. The matching
  (0,1), (2,4r), (3,4), (5,6), ..., (4r-3,4r-2)
has 2r disjoint edges and leaves one vertex unmatched. An independent set
has at most one vertex per matching edge plus that last vertex, so 2r+1
is the exact independence number. This elementary bound avoids needing
any classification of independent sets.

The reflection length is odd: it is conjugate to a simple reflection, or
equivalently has determinant -1 in the faithful geometric representation.
The product i(I_r) has a (-1)-eigenspace of dimension 2r+1 since its simple
roots are orthogonal; a real-root reflection has dimension one. They cannot
coincide. The maximum-descents lemma in part 5 then also proves that w is
not fully commutative.

The coordinate beta_(r,k),0 equals r-1+4k+(4r-8)k^2 and is strictly increasing
for k>=0. Positive norm-two roots defining the same reflection must be
equal: its rank-one difference from the identity has their common image
line, and the norm and positivity fix the scalar. Thus the reflections are
distinct for fixed r. Different r label different ranks, not points in a
single fixed-rank family; no cross-rank comparison of lengths is needed.

5. ORDINARY KL CONSEQUENCE AND EVENTUAL VANISHING

Primary structural input: R. M. Green, On the Markov trace for Temperley--
Lieb algebras of type E_n, arXiv:0704.0283, Definition 4.1 and Proposition
4.4(iv),(v); also the final paragraph of Lemma 5.2. This applies to every
generalized E_n, including indefinite cases. The cells used here are
monomial Temperley--Lieb cells. No identification with ordinary KL cells
or ordinary Lusztig a-values is required or made.

If an FC x has a maximum independent set I in BOTH descent sets, i(I)
occurs as both a reduced prefix and suffix. Its monomial a-value is |I|:
the commuting prefix supplies the lower bound, and a larger commuting
factor is impossible by the independence number of the support graph.
Green 4.4(iv) puts x in the right monomial cell of i(I), and its left-right
analogue puts it in the left monomial cell of i(I). Green 4.4(v) says their
intersection is a singleton, which already contains i(I). Thus x=i(I).

Now take any FC x<w and gap>1. If an upper left or right descent is absent
from the corresponding lower descent set, the ordinary KL descent identity
forces mu(x,w)=0; the only exceptional nonzero case of that identity is a
cover. Otherwise the preceding lemma forces x=i(I_r), of odd length 2r+1.
Since w is also odd, the gap is even and its ordinary leading coefficient
is zero. Every cover has mu=1. This proves the 0/1 assertion and vanishing
for every non-cover, uniformly without computing P. Non-comparable x have
mu=0 by convention, if the statement is extended to all FC elements.

For a fixed r, Stembridge's FC-finiteness classification gives a finite
maximum length M_r of an FC element. Because each bounded Coxeter length
ball is finite and k->w_(r,k) is injective, only finitely many k satisfy
ell(w_(r,k))<=M_r+1. Therefore for every sufficiently large k there are no
FC lower covers; all remaining coefficients were already zero. This is
the asserted eventual all-zero result. It gives no explicit threshold,
no threshold uniform in r, and no monotonic or closed-form length formula.

6. AUDIT OF THE PROPOSED TWO-DIFFERENT-DESCENT-SETS STRENGTHENING

This extension is valid as a separate lemma. Let K be a support vertex
set in a generalized E diagram, and let I,J be MAXIMUM independent subsets
of K, both of cardinality alpha(K). Define
  x*=i(I)i(J\I),   ell(x*)=2 alpha(K)-|I intersect J|.
The displayed word uses each generator at most once. It is reduced and FC;
no commutations can create a repeated-letter braid. Every element of
I intersect J commutes with every generator in I union J, so the displayed
word can also be written with i(J) as a suffix. Hence I is contained in its
left descents and J in its right descents. Both containments are equalities
because FC descent sets are independent and I,J are maximum.

For any FC x supported in K with I contained in L(x) and J contained in R(x),
a_TL(x)=alpha(K), and the same holds for x*. By Green 4.4(iv), x and x* lie
in the right cell of i(I) and the left cell of i(J). These cells belong to
the same two-sided cell because x* lies in their intersection. Part (v)
then forces x=x*. Thus, for an upper b with these descent sets, parity
  ell(b) congruent to |I intersect J| modulo 2
forces all non-cover FC leading coefficients to vanish. Covers remain 1.
If x* is not below b, there is simply no eligible lower endpoint; no claim
that x*<=b is required. The proof requires maximum independent sets in
the SAME support graph, not merely inclusion-maximal independent sets.

7. PRECISE LIMITATIONS

The invariant mu is one specified leading coefficient, which is zero by
parity for an even interval rank. Its vanishing does not eliminate lower
degree terms of P. Even a theorem controlling every FC-lower mu for one
fixed upper does not by itself give P=1, because KL recursion also uses
different uppers and lower-degree contributions. No constant-polynomial
claim follows from the maximum-descent argument, from arbitrarily large
upper lengths, or from eventual all-zero mu. The audited TeX says this
explicitly and correctly.

The current uniform construction covers ranks n=4r+1, r>=3. It is stronger
than embedding one fixed example into higher ranks because every upper has
full support in its own growing diagram. It remains an explicit family,
not a whole-type statement for arbitrary upper endpoints in infinite E_n.
Historical novelty remains unconfirmed and is not certified by this report.
