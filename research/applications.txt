APPLICATIONS OF GERN'S TYPE-D RESULTS AFTER FULL COMBINATORIAL INVARIANCE

Research notes, 2026-10-06 (America/Chicago). These notes distinguish a consequence of the supplied
2026 invariance theorem from a result that genuinely needed that theorem. No
novelty claim is made for parabolic-coset transport, which is classical.

1. Parabolic cosets give explicit families (classical transport).

Let (W,S) be any Coxeter system, J a subset of S, and a in W^J a minimal
representative for a right coset a W_J. For x <= y in W_J, multiplication
z |-> a z gives an order isomorphism [x,y]_{W_J} -> [a x,a y]_W.

Proof: Bruhat order restricted to a W_J agrees, under this multiplication,
with Bruhat order on W_J; this is the standard parabolic subword lemma.
The parabolic projection pi_J: W -> W^J is order preserving. An element z
between a x and a y has a <= pi_J(z) <= a, hence belongs to a W_J. Thus the
image is the entire interval, not merely an induced subposet. The length
difference is preserved because l(a z)=l(a)+l(z) for z in W_J.

Consequently, if W_J has type D_n and x is fully commutative in W_J, then
mu_W(a x,a y) belongs to {0,1}. The ambient lower endpoint a x need not be
fully commutative. In fact P^W_{a x,a y}=P^{W_J}_{x,y} was already a standard
parabolic property; it follows by induction with the right KL recurrence
using upper descents in J. Terms between a x and a y stay in the coset.
Do not call this preservation a new consequence unavailable before 2026.

2. An explicit infinite affine family whose lower endpoints are not FC.

Take W of affine type D_n, n>=4, and J the finite D_n nodes. Use the affine
root convention t_lambda(alpha+k delta)=alpha+(k-<lambda,alpha>) delta.
Let rho^vee be the half-sum of positive finite coroots and m>=1. Then
lambda_m=-2m rho^vee is in the coroot lattice and a_m=t_{lambda_m} belongs
to W^J: a_m(alpha_j)=alpha_j+2m delta is positive for every finite simple
root alpha_j. On the other hand a_m^{-1}(alpha_j)=alpha_j-2m delta is
negative, so every j in J is a LEFT descent of a_m.

Since D_n has adjacent simple nodes, its full simple descent set is not
commutative. Thus a_m is not fully commutative (the longest rank-two
parabolic element for any adjacent pair is a reduced left factor).
For every x in W_J, a_m x has a reduced expression formed by concatenating
a reduced expression of a_m with one of x. Therefore a_m x is not FC.

For any FC x in D_n and arbitrary y>=x in D_n,
  mu_affine-D_n(t_{-2m rho^vee} x, t_{-2m rho^vee} y) is 0 or 1.
For Gern's distinguished pair (x_n,w_n), it equals 1 for n=6 or 7 and 0
for every other n>=4. All degree bounds for P_{x_n,w_n} carry over too.
The endpoints have unbounded absolute length as m grows; their interval
rank is fixed. This is a genuine infinite family of non-FC lower endpoints,
but its transport mechanism is classical, not an application establishing
the whole affine-D fully-commutative-bottom conjecture.

3. Explicit finite exceptional examples carrying the rank-11 bad interval.

Use E7/E8 numbering with edges 1--3--4--5--6--7[--8] and 2--4. Let
J={2,3,4,5,6,7}; its induced diagram is D6. Identify Gern's D6 generators
(s1,s2,s3,s4,s5,s6) with (r2,r3,r4,r5,r6,r7). Write x and w for the
images of his x6 and w6. They have lengths 4 and 15 and mu(x,w)=1.
Let a=w0(E_k) w0(J), for k=7 or 8. The longest element of E7/E8 acts as
-1 on roots. Thus a maps every positive root of J to a positive root;
in particular a is minimal in a W_J. Its length is 63-30=33 in E7 and
120-30=90 in E8. Hence
  E7: l(a x)=37, l(a w)=48, mu(a x,a w)=1;
  E8: l(a x)=94, l(a w)=105, mu(a x,a w)=1.
Both intervals have rank 11 and are isomorphic to Gern's [x6,w6].

The lower endpoints here are NOT fully commutative. Exact integer root
certificates are reproduced by research/exceptional_cosets.py.
In E7, right multiplying a successively by
  r1,r3,r4,r2,r5,r4,r3,r1,r6,r5,r4,r2,r3,r4,r5
reduces length at every step. The remainder b has right descents r6,r7:
  b(alpha6)=-(alpha1+alpha3+alpha4+alpha5+alpha6+alpha7),
  b(alpha7)=-(alpha1+2alpha2+2alpha3+3alpha4+2alpha5+alpha6).
So b has a reduced suffix r6 r7 r6, and a has a reduced expression
containing that braid. In E8 the shorter stripping sequence
  r1,r3,r4,r2,r5,r4,r3,r1,r6
leaves right descents r7,r8, with images
  -(alpha1+2alpha2+2alpha3+3alpha4+2alpha5+alpha6),
  -(alpha2+alpha3+2alpha4+2alpha5+2alpha6+2alpha7+alpha8).
Again a is not FC, and concatenation proves a x is not FC.

These examples concretely extend the endpoint class beyond FC type D and
give E7/E8 occurrences of the exceptional coefficient 1. They are not
new computations in the strong sense: parabolic-coset transport proves
them without the supplied full invariance theorem.

4. Products yield interior coefficient bounds, not only a mu bound.

Assume full combinatorial invariance as in the supplied 2026 Theorem 1.1.
Let an ambient interval [u,v] be isomorphic as an abstract poset to a
product of k nontrivial Bruhat intervals [x_i,y_i] in finite D types with
each x_i fully commutative. Put d_i=l(y_i)-l(x_i), d=sum d_i.
The product is itself the interval from (x_i) to (y_i) in the product
Coxeter group. Standard tensor-product factorization of the Hecke algebra
gives
  P_{u,v}(q)=prod_i P_{x_i,y_i}(q).
Therefore
  deg P_{u,v} <= sum_i floor((d_i-1)/2).
For k>=2 this is strictly below (d-1)/2, so mu(u,v)=0.

If every d_i is odd, the coefficient of q^{(d-k)/2} is
  prod_i mu(x_i,y_i),
and hence is 0 or 1 by Gern. This bounds a coefficient below the universal
top slot, a statement not supplied by the single-interval mu bound alone.
For an interval isomorphic to I6^k, where I6=[x6,w6], the degree is exactly
5k and the coefficient at that degree is 1 (since mu(x6,w6)=1 and rank11).
The interval rank is 11k. For k>=2 its mu is zero, even though the actual
leading coefficient is 1. Any proposed finite/affine exceptional example
requires a full poset isomorphism or a proved product decomposition.

Novelty qualification: Hecke-algebra direct-product factorization is old.
Full invariance permits its use on an arbitrary abstract product
decomposition across ambient groups. Whether the same abstract-product
statement had already been proved in a narrower setting needs a dedicated
literature check. Avoid presenting it as newly discovered.

5. Literature cautions and failed shortcuts.

Michael S. Chmutov's 2014 Michigan dissertation, The structure of W-graphs
arising in Kazhdan-Lusztig theory, explicitly proves Green's 0-1 conjecture
in type B (abstract and Chapter 2, Section 2.3). Thus a full FC-bottom
finite B/C bound is not a new application of Gern plus the 2026 theorem.
Primary source URL:
https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/108802/mchmutov_1.pdf?sequence=1
The repository fetch returned an HTML challenge; no fake PDF was retained.
The primary source is indexed by web search, and author-uploaded full text
also appears at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295404674.
Green's affine-A result is https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.1650.

An inclusion of root systems D_n inside B_n is insufficient. Bruhat order
and Coxeter lengths depend on the ambient simple system; a reflection
subgroup is not automatically an interval-preserving embedding. One must
prove surjectivity onto the full ambient interval, not only an order
embedding or agreement of endpoints' ranks.

Likewise a root-subsystem pattern occurrence, or a flattened permutation,
does not alone give equality of KL polynomials. Standard interval pattern
embeddings with verified length condition may give such equality already
by older geometric results, so they should not automatically be promoted
as new consequences of full abstract invariance.

No proof was found that every FC-bottom interval in exceptional E or
affine D admits an FC-bottom finite-D realization. Such a claim would
require a new realization theorem, and it is not a consequence of the
2026 invariance theorem itself.

criterion, negative-root left-descent argument, and length-additive

7. Second audit: products and the explicit B3 example.

The updated product theorem in results/kl-transfer.tex is correct. The
degree estimate is strict at the mu exponent for k>=2; even total rank
already makes mu zero by definition. For all odd factor ranks, only the
product of their largest allowed terms can reach exponent (d-k)/2, so
Gern's bound applies exactly as written. F(1)=26 is correct.

The displayed B3 example agrees entry-by-entry with the JSON certificate:
source [s3,s3 s1 s2 s3] in Gern D4 numbering, target bottom (3,2,-1),
target top (2,-1,-3), polynomial 1+q, rank vector (1,4,4,1). Its bottom
word b0 b1 b2 b1 is reduced and contains the m(b1,b2)=3 braid.
The two right labels used in the type-A-coset exclusion can be verified
from the actual covers
  (3,2,-1) < (1,2,-3): label (-3,2,-1),
  (3,2,-1) < (-3,2,-1): label (-1,2,3).
Their product has order four. The argument works for conjugate type-A
parabolics too: T(W) intersect H equals the reflection set T(H) for a
parabolic subgroup H, including conjugates. Thus an ambient reflection
belonging to H is a type-A reflection, and two such cannot have product
order four. The left-label argument excludes right cosets analogously.

8. A proved all-rank forbidden pattern for finite D realizations.

Define L4 to be the eight-element graded poset with ranks
  {a}, {b1,b2}, {c1,c2}, {d1,d2}, {z},
and every possible order relation between consecutive ranks. This is
exactly the Bruhat poset [e,w0] in I2(4)=B2.

PROPOSITION. No Bruhat interval in a finite simply-laced Weyl group is
isomorphic to L4. In particular, no interval in any finite D_n has this
poset, with or without a fully commutative bottom. More generally, any
Bruhat interval containing a closed subinterval isomorphic to L4 has no
realization as a Bruhat interval in any finite simply-laced Weyl group.

PROOF. Work in the real Euclidean reflection representation of a finite
simply-laced Weyl group. For distinct reflections r_alpha,r_beta,
  im(r_alpha r_beta - 1)=span(alpha,beta).
Indeed the product fixes the orthogonal complement pointwise and acts
on that plane as a nonidentity rotation. Its restriction minus the
identity is invertible. Consequently, if two distinct-reflection
factorizations represent the same group element, their root planes agree.

Suppose an interval with the displayed layers existed. Every cover
p<q has a left reflection label t with q=t p. Let Pi be the plane of
the two distinct labels of a<b1 and a<b2. For each cj, the two paths
a<b1<cj and a<b2<cj give two factorizations of cj a^{-1} into distinct
reflections. The common-plane identity implies that the labels of both
paths lie in Pi: the shared plane contains both distinct first labels,
so it must be Pi. Thus all four b-to-c edge labels lie in Pi.

Next use the two paths b1<c1<dj and b1<c2<dj for each dj. Their distinct
first labels already lie in Pi and span it. The same identity forces
their last labels into Pi; using b2 similarly covers the other labels.
Finally paths c1<d1<z and c1<d2<z put the top edge labels in Pi. Every
vertex is therefore obtained from a by left multiplication by reflections
whose roots lie in Pi. All eight vertices lie in one coset of the group
generated by the roots Phi intersect Pi.

But Phi intersect Pi is a rank-two root subsystem in a finite simply-laced
crystallographic root system. It is A1 x A1 or A2, and its reflection group
has order four or six. A coset cannot contain eight distinct vertices.
This is the desired contradiction. If a larger interval contained L4,
any claimed interval isomorphism would restrict to an impossible L4
interval in the target. QED.

This proof is independent of full KL combinatorial invariance. It also
follows immediately from the supplied preprint's Proposition 3.5 on
transport of dihedral intervals, but invoking that result is unnecessary
for the finite simply-laced conclusion. No novelty claim has been checked.
It legitimately upgrades the program's bounded D4 absence observation
to all D_n; the program itself should still report only its tested scope.

It is a different obstruction from the coefficient test: P_{e,w0}=1 in
finite B2, yet its interval has no finite-D realization. It also proves
that a blanket claim 'every interval with mu<=1 has an FC-bottom D model'
is false. The conclusion does not apply to infinite simply-laced groups,
whose maximal rank-two reflection subgroups can be infinite dihedral.

Primary historical reference located after writing the proof:
  Matthew Dyer, On the "Bruhat graph" of a Coxeter system,
  Compositio Mathematica 78 (1991), 185--191.
  https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1991__78_2_185_0.pdf
Lemma 3.1 (p.188) proves that two nontrivial two-reflection factorizations
of the same element generate a dihedral reflection subgroup, using the
image-of-product-minus-identity plane argument. Section 3.4 (p.190)
recovers a maximal dihedral coset intersection by closure under four-cycle
moves, and explicitly observes that an interval with exactly two atoms
or exactly two coatoms is isomorphic to an interval in a dihedral group.
Thus the obstruction is a consequence of classical reflection-subgroup
geometry, with our direct proof supplied for convenience. These are
antecedents, not support for a priority claim.
