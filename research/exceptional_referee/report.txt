Verdict

I find no mathematical or implementation error blocking the proposed theorem:
for ordinary equal-parameter Kazhdan--Lusztig coefficients in finite type E6
or E7, mu(x,w) belongs to {0,1} whenever x is fully commutative.

The supplied argument, including research/broad_exceptional/e6_e7_proof.tex,
is a valid computer-assisted proof when its executable terminal classification
is included as part of the evidence. It is stronger than a sample of tested
intervals: a mathematical reduction handles every upper endpoint, and the

makes no claim about historical priority or whether the theorem is new.

1. Reduction and order conventions

Induction on the upper length is sound. First dispose of x not strictly below
w, even length differences, and length difference one. These have values 0,
0, and 1, respectively. Hence the substantive induction step has odd gap
d >= 3.

Gern Proposition 1.2.4(3),(4) then allows the proof to assume
L(w) subset L(x) and R(w) subset R(x). The exceptional value 1 in the missing-
descent criterion occurs only for a cover, already excluded. Fully commutative
elements have commuting descent sets. Therefore both descent sets of w are
commuting as well. In particular, an upper endpoint with noncommuting descent
set has already been handled; no additional classification is needed for it.

If w is not weakly bad, a reduced expression starts st or ends ts with
m(s,t)=3. In the first case, s is a left descent of w and t is not, since
L(w) commutes. The left star is sw: it has t but not s as a left descent and
has length one smaller. The descent inclusion and full commutativity of x
put x in the same star domain. Its star remains fully commutative. The right
case is identical after inversion.

The draft correctly uses the symmetric star-transport identity. This matters:
the unqualified oriented identity as printed in Gern Proposition 1.2.4 is
unsafe when order reverses. In type A2, for example, the left star exchanges
the ordered pair (s,ts) with (ts,s); the ordinary oriented mu values are 1
and 0. The proposed proof excludes exactly this difficulty. For d >= 3,
the starred length gap is d or d-2, so remains positive. A starred pair is
either in the original order or incomparable; in the latter case its
symmetric coefficient is zero. The induction hypothesis therefore applies
to the strictly shorter upper endpoint whenever necessary. No assertion
that stars preserve the full Bruhat interval or KL polynomial is used.

2. Citation and scope audit

The local primary text sources/gern-thesis-2013.txt supports the needed
statements: Definition 1.1.25 defines the length-three stars for simply laced
systems; Proposition 1.1.29 gives full-commutativity preservation; Proposition
1.2.4 gives the descent and star identities; Corollary 1.2.5 handles products
of commuting generators; Theorem 4.5.11 handles arbitrary upper endpoints
in type D with fully commutative lower endpoints.

Proposition 1.1.29 has an obvious variable typo: its conclusion prints *w
although its hypothesis names x. It is the statement about *x that is used.
The finite E6/E7 computations independently check full commutativity at every
left and right length-three star move, so this application also has explicit
finite verification (3,620 moves in E6; 16,776 in E7).

Do not invoke Gern Proposition 1.1.28, Corollary 2.3.11, or the type-D bad-
element classification as if their scope included E types. The current draft
does not do so: its direct reduction and exhaustive terminal classification
replace precisely that missing step. Explicit references to Proposition
1.1.29 and Proposition 1.2.4 should be added to the reduction paragraph in
the final integrated document.

The type-D appeal uses standard-parabolic invariance of ordinary KL
polynomials. This is standard and distinct from general combinatorial
invariance. For clarity, state explicitly that P^E7_{x,b}=P^D6_{x,b} when
both endpoints lie in this standard D6 parabolic. Full commutativity of x
also restricts to the parabolic. No new combinatorial-invariance theorem is
needed for the proof.

4. Exact-enumeration audit

I reviewed exact_e.py, enumerate_bad.cpp, verify_e6_independent.py, and
verify_e7_matrices.cpp. The root-index and matrix representations are faithful
simple-root-image representations. Breadth-first closure under every simple
reflection enumerates all group elements, with minimal word lengths. Hash
collisions do not identify unequal elements: the maps also test key equality.
The enumeration is not truncated at a chosen length or constrained support.

The matrix multiplication formula is correct: negate column s and add its
old value to each adjacent column. Left actions and left descents are
obtained correctly from inversion. The E7 int8_t matrix storage is safe:
the E7 root coefficients have absolute value at most 4. My auxiliary check
independently closes the roots and confirms 72 roots / bound 3 for E6 and
126 roots / bound 4 for E7. Intermediate additions are evaluated as int.

The full-commutativity recurrence is correct in simply laced type. A forbidden
length-three braid before the last letter persists in a shorter reduced
prefix. A forbidden braid at the last letter supplies adjacent right descents
by the braid relation. Conversely, a non-FC reduced prefix remains non-FC
after a reduced extension, and adjacent right descents give a braid suffix.
Thus checking every descending predecessor and a commuting right descent
set is an exact characterization, not a heuristic pattern test.

The weakly-bad test is exact: s in R(w) and adjacent t in R(ws) is equivalent
to a reduced suffix ts. Inversion supplies the analogous prefix criterion.
Every weakly-bad fully commutative element is explicitly checked to be a
product of distinct mutually commuting generators. The eligible bottoms are
searched over the complete FC list.

The greedy Bruhat comparison is the standard lifting recurrence. For a
right descent s of w, replace w by ws, and replace x by xs exactly when s
is a right descent of x. Reject only after the lower length is at least the
upper length and the elements differ. The identity case cannot reach the
zero-descent bit scan. I found no ordering, initialization, or indexing bug.

The two engines are independently implemented representations, but share
the same mathematical FC and Bruhat recurrences. Calling them independent
implementations is accurate; calling them wholly independent algorithms
would overstate the independence. Their common recurrences have been
justified mathematically above.
