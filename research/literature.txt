MAIN FINDINGS

1. Whole-type B/C novelty is ruled out by Chmutov (2014).
   Michael S. Chmutov, The structure of W-graphs arising in Kazhdan-Lusztig theory,
   PhD thesis, University of Michigan, 2014, §2.3, Theorem 2.3.8, printed p.58;
   proof printed pp.61–62. Section 2.3 explicitly fixes W of type B_n. The theorem
   states that x<w and x fully commutative imply mu(x,w) in {0,1}, with arbitrary w.
   Hence it also covers equal-parameter C_n, the same Coxeter system. This is a
   direct successor to Green's affine-A result and Gern's D result. It uses transport
   across the double bond, plus Green's earlier both-FC result.
   Primary repository: https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/108802
   Primary PDF: https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/108802/mchmutov_1.pdf?sequence=1
   The repository blocked direct download (403), but its indexed primary PDF
   returned the exact theorem and page. The author-uploaded full thesis text was
   independently inspected at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295404674_The_Structure_of_W-graphs_Arising_in_Kazhdan-Lusztig_Theory

2. A/affine-A are already known; be precise about versions of Green's theorem.
   R.M. Green, Leading coefficients of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials and fully
   commutative elements, J. Algebraic Combin. 30 (2009), 165–171,
   DOI 10.1007/s10801-008-0156-x, PUBLISHED Theorem 4.1 (Remark 4.2 for n=2).
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-008-0156-x
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.1650
   arXiv v1 has the same main theorem numbered 3.1, with Remark 3.2 for n=2.
   Gern's citation to Green Theorem 3.1 follows the preprint numbering. The result
   assumes x fully commutative, w arbitrary in affine A_(n-1), and includes finite
   A by standard parabolic restriction. The introduction suggests broad generality
   as computational evidence, not a proof in all Coxeter systems.

3. Gern's input is exactly as advertised, but all original type-D numerics are old.
   Tyson C. Gern, Leading Coefficients of Kazhdan–Lusztig Polynomials in Type D,
   revised PhD thesis, University of Colorado Boulder, 2013, arXiv:1304.6074v1.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.6074
   Theorem 4.5.11, printed p.66: x fully commutative and w arbitrary in D_n gives
   mu(x,w) in {0,1}.
   Proposition 4.5.1, printed p.63: a(w_n)=3n/4 if n=0 mod4, (3n+2)/4 if n=2 mod4.
   Lemma 2.2.20 gives ell(w_n)=3n^2/8+n/4 for even n.
   Lemmas 4.5.2–4.5.3, printed pp.63–64: large even bad-pair mu vanishes by
   degree bounds using P_(e,w_n), a(w_n), and Lemma 2.3.9.
   Lemma 4.5.4: mu(x_4,w_4)=0 by parity.
   Lemma 4.5.5: mu(x_6,w_6)=1; mu(x_8,w_8)=0, computed with du Cloux's Coxeter.
   Lemma 4.5.6: odd n>=5 reduces to n-1 with the same reduced expressions.
   Lemma 4.5.7: all exceptional bad pairs have 0/1 mu.
   Lemma 4.5.9: lower-FC/arbitrary-bad-top reduction.
   Gern explicitly notes on printed p.26 that n=2 or4 mod8 forces even interval
   rank, hence mu=0 without the difficult calculation.

4. Both-endpoint results must not be conflated with the lower-endpoint theorem.
   Green, Generalized Jones traces and Kazhdan–Lusztig bases,
   J. Pure Appl. Algebra 211 (2007), no.3, 744–772,
   DOI 10.1016/j.jpaa.2007.04.004; https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0509362 .
   arXiv v2 Theorem 7.10 gives a trace computation under Properties B,F and a
   bipartite Coxeter graph. Remark 7.12 explains the simply-laced both-FC 0/1
   property (and cites Graham for ADE); Remark 7.13 explicitly asserts both-FC
   0/1 for B and H_n, even the infinite-rank family of H-shaped diagrams.
   These are old results, but do not alone cover lower FC/arbitrary upper.
   Chmutov explicitly uses the B both-FC result to start his proof.

5. Upper-Deodhar results also have different hypotheses.
   Brant C. Jones, Leading coefficients of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for Deodhar
   elements, J. Algebraic Combin. 29 (2009), 229–260,
   DOI 10.1007/s10801-008-0131-6; https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.1391 .
   Theorem 1.2: upper w Deodhar in a finite Weyl group, arbitrary lower x => 0/1.
   Theorem 6.1 explicitly covers E6,E7,E8,F4,G2 by finite checks; Theorem 5.4 covers
   D, and the B corollary is numbered 4.2 in arXiv and 4.3 in the published copy.
   Deodhar is a subclass of fully commutative. Gern's bad tops are not FC
   (Corollary 2.3.7), so these do not directly handle his bad pairs.

6. Exceptional/affine status: do not turn search non-results into an open-problem claim.
   I did not locate a primary source proving the complete lower-FC/arbitrary-upper
   0/1 assertion in affine D or all E,F,H. Found prior results concern both FC,
   upper Deodhar, cell structure, FC enumeration, or general large mu examples.
   Thus no defensible claim that those whole types are settled by pure invariance
   unless an actual interval realization/classification is proved. Equally, this
   audit does not certify that their full bounds remain open in October 2026.
   Pan Chen, Non-trivial Kazhdan-Lusztig coefficients of affine Weyl groups,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02353 , supplies coefficients n in affine B_n and
   n+1 in affine C_n,D_n. Its abstract imposes no FC-bottom restriction. These
   are NOT verified counterexamples to Green's FC conjecture. General 0/1 is false
   already in A (McLarnan–Warrington), but that too does not refute FC-bottom 0/1.

OLD TRANSPORT RESULTS THAT LIMIT NOVELTY

7. Subsystem interval-pattern embeddings already preserve the entire polynomial.
   Alexander Woo, Interval pattern avoidance for arbitrary root systems,
   Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 53 (2010), 757–762,
   DOI 10.4153/CMB-2010-080-2; https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611328 .
   Published Corollary 3.3; arXiv v1 Corollary 2. If a subsystem embedding embeds
   [u,v] into [x,w], then P_(u,v)=P_(x,w). Published Theorem 3.1 (arXiv Theorem1)
   is the isomorphism of Richardson varieties underlying it. Definition requires
   endpoint flattenings, common subsystem coset, and interval isomorphism;
   with the first two, equality of interval lengths suffices for the last.
   https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/85C1445519680BA9B1E25DB940388A75/S0008439500017380a.pdf/interval-pattern-avoidance-for-arbitrary-root-systems.pdf
   Do not claim any such transfer first follows from the September 2026 input.
   Woo–Yong's type-A precursor: Governing singularities of Schubert varieties,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0603273 .

8. Reflection subgroups alone are insufficient.
   Dyer's reflection-subgroup theory gives the induced directed Bruhat graph in a
   coset; it does not identify all ambient interval vertices with a subgroup
   interval. EMS (below), Theorem 2.2, cites Dyer 1990 Theorem3.3/Cor3.4 and
   Dyer 1991 Theorem1.4 for the labelled graph statement. Its Lemma3.1 discussion
   explicitly cautions that the inverse to the coset graph map need not preserve
   ambient Bruhat order. A claimed transfer needs convexity/full interval equality,
   or an independently certified interval-poset isomorphism.
   Dyer, Reflection subgroups of Coxeter systems, J. Algebra 135 (1990), 57–73.
   Dyer, On the Bruhat graph of a Coxeter system, Compos. Math. 78 (1991), 185–191.
   Dyer, Hecke algebras and shellings of Bruhat intervals, Compos. Math.89 (1993),91–115.
   These locators are also accessible via EMS §2.2:
   https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16433v2#S2.SS2

9. FC is NOT recoverable from abstract lower Bruhat intervals across Coxeter types.
   Elementary counterexample (direct proof, not attributed novelty): compare
   [e,sts] in I2(3) and I2(4). Both have ranks of sizes 1,2,2,1 and every possible
   consecutive-layer comparability. In I2(3), sts=tst is non-FC. In I2(4), sts is
   FC because the only noncommuting braid has length4, longer than this element.
   Thus even an isomorphism between principal LOWER intervals does not preserve
   the full commutativity of their tops. A fortiori [x,w] alone cannot preserve a
   bottom's FC property: every cover interval is a 2-chain, with FC or non-FC
   bottom available. The conditional certificate should require a type-D model
   with FC lower endpoint, not assert that the target endpoint is FC.
   Stembridge's distributive-lattice characterization is for WEAK order, not full
   strong Bruhat order. On the fully commutative elements of Coxeter groups,
   J. Algebraic Combin. 5 (1996),353–385, Theorem3.2; heap criterion Prop2.3.
   https://www2.math.ethz.ch/EMIS/journals/JACO/Volume5_4/cq9j136368033131.fulltext.pdf

10. Existing partial invariance must be versioned and scoped carefully.
   Grant T. Barkley, Christian Gaetz, Thomas Lam,
   Combinatorial invariance for the coefficient of q in Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials,
   arXiv:2601.07793v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07793v2 .
   Corollary1.3: coefficient of q invariant in all Coxeter groups, plus corresponding
   R and R-tilde coefficient statements. Corollary1.4: complete invariance for
   intervals of rank<=6 in arbitrary Coxeter groups.
   BGL here means Barkley–Gaetz–Lam, NOT Brenti–Godelle–Libedinsky.
   Francesco Esposito, Mario Marietti, Salvatore Stella,
   Flip Combinatorial Invariance and Weyl groups,
   arXiv:2509.16433v2 (17 Sep 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16433v2 .
   Corollary1.3(ii): full invariance up to length10 between finite Weyl-group
   intervals, and up to length12 when BOTH realizations have type A.
   v1 search snippets still say 8 and10; those are superseded by v2.
   Corollary1.3(i): R-tilde congruence modulo q^7 for Weyl groups, modulo q^8 for A.
   Theorem1.2 says flip invariance fails in H3,H4; this is NOT failure of KL
   combinatorial invariance and NOT failure of an FC mu bound.
   Earlier classical bounds: Federico Incitti, More on the combinatorial invariance
   of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials, JCTA114 (2007),461–482,
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2006.06.009 : A rank<=8, B,D rank<=6.

11. Other old invariant subclasses need checking for any explicit I_n.
   Principal lower intervals: Brenti–Caselli–Marietti (2006), Special matchings and
   Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials, Adv.Math202,555–601,
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2005.01.011 ; Delanoy (2006), Combinatorial
   invariance of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials on intervals starting from the identity,
   JAC24,437–463, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-006-0014-7 .
   Marietti extends to parabolic lower intervals for arbitrary Coxeter groups,
   Adv.Math335 (2018),180–210, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2018.07.008 .
   Lattice intervals are also an older invariant class (Dyer thesis §7.23 and
   Brenti, A combinatorial formula for Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials, Invent.Math118
   (1994),371–394; identified in EMS introduction). These cases must be ruled out
   or acknowledged if one says a particular bad interval required full invariance.
   Caselli–Marietti, Special Matchings, Brenti's Conjecture, and the Combinatorial
   Invariance Conjecture, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11776v1 , Theorem8.6 gives
   special-matching recursion on arbitrary TYPE-A intervals. Its algorithm for
   arbitrary type-A intervals remains conjectural (Conjecture8.13/Prop8.15).
   Historical conjectures relating nonzero mu and special matchings cannot simply
   be invoked as proved D-to-arbitrary-group transport:
   https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~slc/s/s49brenti.pdf (Conjecture31).

BAD-INTERVAL CLAIM: DEFENSIBLE FORM AND NUMERICAL CALIBRATION

12. Let I_n=[x_n,w_n] in Gern's D_n, n even>=4. If the supplied OpenAI Theorem1.1
   is accepted, every interval [u,v] in any Coxeter system isomorphic to I_n has
   the same full polynomial and hence the same mu and degree. This is a formal
   conditional corollary. It is stronger in scope than old root-subsystem pattern
   transport but does not itself construct any new realization or solve an entire
   type. The D_n source computation and a-value are Gern's. The target a-function
   need not equal the source a-value: transfer the resulting numerical polynomial
   degree bound, not a(w) itself.

13. For ell(w_n)=3n²/8+n/4, ell(x_n)=n/2+1,
       d_n=3n²/8-n/4-1,
       B_n=min(floor((d_n-1)/2), floor((ell(w_n)-a(w_n))/2)).
   Gern's P_(x_n,w_n)=P_(e,w_n) reduction and a-bound produce B_n in the source.
   At n=8, mu=0 further improves the bound to9, because d_8=21 and its top
   allowable coefficient [q^10] vanishes. Formula alone gives10.
   Numerical comparison (not a claim of sharpness):
     n     d       ordinary bound   B_n       refined if n8
     4      4        1               1
     6     11        5               5
     8     21       10              10         9
    10     34       16              16
    12     50       24              24
    14     69       34              33
    16     91       45              44
   First generic strict improvement of B_n over ordinary bound occurs n=14;
   n=8 is the earlier exceptional strict improvement.
   mu is1 only n6; it is0 otherwise. Odd n duplicates the preceding even n,
   so mu1 also at n7 if that redundant indexing is included.
   For n=2,4 mod8, d_n is even and mu0 is automatic in any realization; it does
   not require Gern or the new theorem. Substantive mu transport concerns the
   odd-rank cases n=0,6 mod8, with n6 positive and all n>=8 zero.

14. Relation to prior short-interval results:
   I_4 rank4 already falls under arbitrary short invariance. I_6 rank11 is above
   EMS's Weyl bound10. The type-A bound12 is inapplicable to a comparison with
   the type-D source unless another type-A model with independently known value
   is supplied. I_8 rank21 and later cases are well beyond those numeric ranges.
   This only shows that the numerical range of those cited general theorems does
   not cover them; it does not exclude old lattice/lower-interval/subsystem results
   or other proofs for individual I_n. No primary source specifically proving
   arbitrary abstract-realization invariance of Gern's whole bad-interval family
   was found in this search. Phrase this as a search result, not proof of novelty.

INDEPENDENT DRAFT AUDIT (results/kl-transfer.tex; 2026-10-07 UTC)

16. Proof audit, including the later product statement.
    The witness theorem correctly combines graded rank preservation with equality
    of the whole polynomial; no FC status of the target endpoint is required.
    The obstruction is a valid consequence. The elementary I2(3)/I2(4) example
    correctly separates FC status from abstract principal strong Bruhat order.
    The bad-interval degree theorem uses only source-side a(w_n); it does not
    claim a-function invariance. Both floor operations, the minimum, and the
    exceptional n=8 sharpening are correct. The two residue-class gap formulas
    are algebraically correct: M-A=k-1 for n=8k and M-A=k for n=8k+6.
    The product theorem follows from tensor-product factorization of the KL
    basis; k>=2 nontrivial factors force mu=0 because the degree is at most
    (d-k)/2. With all ranks odd, the specified coefficient is the product of
    the factor mu-values. The computed polynomial F has degree5, leading
    coefficient1, and F(1)=26, so the stated F^k consequence follows.
    The convex-coset proof via order-preserving parabolic projection is valid.
    The recurrence proof of coset KL equality correctly handles zero terms and
    puts every nonzero correction term in the same coset. Its induction is on
    upper parabolic length, so all needed polynomial and mu factors are smaller.
    The affine translation argument has the correct signs for the displayed
    root-action convention; 2rho^vee is in the coroot lattice. Adjacent finite
    left descents give an sts reduced prefix. Length-additive extension to a_mx
    retains a noncommuting braid and hence fails FC.
    I did not independently run/audit the complete finite computation code or
    the exceptional root script: those are separate computational evidence.

17. Corrections/precision recommendations sent to the parent, without editing tex.
    (a) Green arXiv identifier must be0801.1650, not0706.2082; published theorem
        is4.1, arXiv v1 theorem3.1. The parent had corrected these on reread.
    (b) Stembridge DOI in the draft is wrong: replace10.1007/BF00215037 by
        10.1007/BF00193185. Publisher confirms title, JAC5(1996)353–385:
        https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00193185
    (c) The prose after the definition of mu calls it the coefficient at the
        largest allowed degree. This is correct only for odd rank. At even rank,
        mu=0 by convention, while the actual coefficient of the largest allowed
        integer power may be nonzero. Add the odd-rank qualification.
    (d) Avoid calling aW_J a right coset while later calling aH a left coset.
        Terminology varies for minimal right parabolic representatives, but the
        unambiguous wording is: a in W^J is the minimal element of aW_J.
    (e) The low-rank table should make the ambient hypotheses explicit: rank<=10
        between finite Weyl realizations, and rank<=12 when both realizations
        are typeA. The following draft paragraph already correctly says that
        the typeA bound does not automatically compare with a typeD source.
    (f) Principal-lower invariance means isomorphisms of principal lower
        intervals (and their subinterval data); it must not be casually read as
        invariance under comparison with any arbitrary interval merely having
        the same abstract shape. No such stronger claim is needed in this note.
