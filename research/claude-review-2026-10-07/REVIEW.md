## 1. Verdict in brief

**The mathematics is correct.** No critical finding survived verification, and nothing refuted a theorem. Every lemma in `results/exceptional-leading.tex` was re-derived by hand against the primary sources, and every computational claim was recomputed from scratch with independent code, in several cases in two independent models and along four different parabolic chains. The finite E6, E7, E8 terminal classification, the D6 polynomial, the uniform family, the affine E9 family, the E10 family, the FC catalogues, and the supplement all check out. The released proof supplement runs cleanly in both modes in under 20 seconds and matches its expected summary byte for byte.

**The paper is not submittable in its current form, for non-mathematical reasons.** It has no author, no acknowledgements, and no disclosure of generative AI use, although the project's own prompts show that the research, proofs, verification, and writing were delegated to AI agents, and the editorial pass deliberately removed the earlier in-text mention of this. The companion note `kl-transfer.tex` names "an Astra research team" in the author field. Every policy checked (arXiv, AMS, Algebraic Combinatorics, Springer Nature) requires in-paper disclosure and a named human author who takes responsibility. The repository and the GitHub release are already public.

**The paper undersells itself in two places and oversells nothing serious.** Theorem uniform can be strengthened from "vanishes for all sufficiently large k" to "vanishes for every k" with a ten-line proof found during this review, and the same argument upgrades the E10 proposition. The paper also omits the single fact that makes the FC hypothesis visibly essential: without it, the leading coefficient reaches 10 already in E6.

**Significance.** The honest classification is a correct, publishable specialist note that completes Green's 2009 question for finite simply laced groups, not an important result. The new mathematics in the finite theorem is the exhaustive terminal classification (where only E8 genuinely needs the pruning method) and the observation that parity disposes of every new exceptional terminal. Nine of the eleven table rows are Gern's type-D bad elements in parabolics; the paper says this only for two of them. The infinite families are root-theoretic constructions whose coefficient statements follow from Fan's cell theorem plus parity.

**Novelty.** A deep search found no prior proof of the exceptional FC-lower bound, no prior classification of T-avoiding elements in type E (Laird's 2016 thesis lists it as open, and the advisor's 2026 CV shows no sequel), and no prior infinite terminal families in affine or indefinite E. The manuscript's hedge is, if anything, more cautious than the evidence warrants. What is missing is context: the result settles what Chmutov's thesis calls "Green's 0-1 conjecture" for finite simply laced type, and infinite families of T-avoiding elements inside a fixed infinite group were already known in affine A, affine C, and type F.

Severity tally after adversarial verification: 0 critical, 5 major, 40 moderate, 46 minor, 42 notes, 0 refuted, 0 disputed. Several verifiers lowered severities; the lists below use the adjusted level.

## 3. Mathematical improvements found during review

These are not errors. They are results the review established that the paper should incorporate.

**3.1 Theorem uniform holds for every k, not only for large k.** (`followup-uniform-all-k`, confirmed by two verifiers.) Let b = b_{r,k}. By the lifting property, any Bruhat cover x of b other than sb and bs for s in I_r has both descent sets containing I_r, so if FC it equals i(I_r) by Lemma maxI, impossible by parity. For x = bs: s is a left descent of x because b(α_s) ≠ ±α_s, and sbs is the reflection in sβ = β − m_s α_s, a non-simple positive root. A non-simple reflection in a simply laced Coxeter group is never fully commutative (its reduced word w⁻¹tw, with l(r_β) = 2 dp(β) − 1, contains a braid factor). FC is inherited by weak-order prefixes, so bs is not FC, and sb = (bs)⁻¹ likewise. Hence μ(x, b_{r,k}) = 0 for every FC x, every r ≥ 3, every k ≥ 0. The same lemma upgrades Proposition thm:indefinite (E10) to full vanishing. Delete the Stembridge-finiteness paragraph and the "no effective threshold" sentence; update the abstract.

The review also showed that the route the manuscript gestures at, bounding FC lengths, can never work: the maximum FC length of E_{4r+1} is at least 8r²+7r−2 (explicit family verified reduced and FC for all odd n up to 101), while l(b_{r,0}) = 8r²+3, so FC covers cannot be excluded by length even at k = 0. The maximum FC lengths are 55, 66, 78, 92, 106, 121 for E10 through E15, growing quadratically.

The length formula l(b_{r,k}) = 8r²+3+116k holds in all 29 tested cases. The slope 116 = 2·l(T) is proved uniformly by a length-additivity criterion that reduces to a finite check over E8 roots; the constant has a proved upper bound and is computationally verified for r ≤ 10 and r = 12 (29 pairs in the review; r ≤ 8 in the shipped certificate, r = 9, 10, 12 in the full runner mode). It is no longer needed for the theorem.

**3.2 The FC hypothesis is essential, and the paper never says so.** (`followup-e6-e7-global-mu`.) A complete KL table of W(E6) (466 million Bruhat pairs, 6.6 million extremal pairs, 2 seconds) gives maximum μ = 10, attained by 8 pairs, with 1,386 pairs having μ ≥ 2, all with non-FC lower endpoint. Witness in the paper's numbering: x = 01254012010 (length 11, not FC), w = 123012523412301251234012 (length 24), P = 1+13q+59q²+121q³+125q⁴+61q⁵+10q⁶. By parabolic inclusion μ ≥ 10 occurs in E7 and E8, and the D6 parabolic already has μ = 4. Among the 6,431 E6 pairs with FC lower endpoint and nonzero μ, every value is 1, which is a brute-force confirmation of Theorem main in E6 independent of the star argument. One sentence with the witness belongs in the introduction. Also worth a remark: FC on the upper endpoint alone does not suffice (μ = 5 with w fully commutative in E6).

**3.3 The "unresolved" affine E8 odd-gap coefficients are computable, and all lie in {0,1}.** (`followup-e9-odd-gap-pairs`, two independent implementations.) The authors' research notes record a second full-support terminal reflection in affine E8 (root (1,2,3,3,2,2,1,1,2), length 33, descent set {3,5,7,8} of non-maximum size, five eligible FC bottoms) and its two length-34 extensions, with coefficients left "UNRESOLVED" after a 3-million-state cap. Working in the parabolic quotient by W_{R(w)} shrinks the 5.8-million-element ideal to 364,156 cosets and the computation takes 5 seconds. Results: μ = 0 for the length-4 bottom (gap 29) and the two even-gap bottoms; μ = 1 for the two length-6 bottoms (gap 27); μ = 1 for the unique bottom of each length-34 extension (gap 29). The paper should report these, both as evidence for the affine bound and because the length-34 extensions satisfy every hypothesis of Proposition parity except parity and have μ = 1, which shows the parity step cannot be dropped. Translated members (k ≥ 1) remain out of reach, and the computed degrees show the a-function bound cannot settle them.

**3.4 Table 1 is "Gern's list plus two".** (`followup-new-terminals-structure`, confirmed.) Under the paper's own label map, the length-7, 8, and 15 rows are exactly Gern's w4, w4·u, and w6 (Theorem 2.3.6 of the thesis), verified by matrix equality and by exhaustive enumeration of D7. The E8 length-28 row is the E7 length-28 row relabelled. So the only noncommuting terminals not inherited from a proper parabolic are the length-28 (E7) and length-50 (E8) elements. A pre-polish draft had a sentence to this effect that was dropped. State it; it is both attribution and a free exhaustiveness cross-check against Gern.

**3.5 Structure of the two new terminals.** Both are involutions but not reflections: the E7 element is a product of four orthogonal reflections (type 4A1), the E8 element of two (type 2A1, fixed subsystem D6). Both are palindromic layered products of independent-set layers alternating between the two colour classes of the diagram, and the four elements w4, w6, b_7, b_8 form a chain in weak order. Each new element has a length-additive factor w_0(J) with J of type A2 × A1³, giving a(b) ≥ 6 and hence μ = 0 by Lusztig's degree bound without any parity input. A short remark would make the new rows readable and the vanishing robust. Note the critic's premise that w6 is a product of two reflections is wrong: it is 3A1.

**3.6 D8 value recomputed.** (`followup-d8-mu-recompute`.) P(x8,w8) = P(e,w8) = 1+12q+59q²+154q³+233q⁴+221q⁵+147q⁶+70q⁷+20q⁸+2q⁹ in two independent models, so μ(x8,w8) = 0 as Gern states; `kl-transfer.tex` can replace its "inherited, not reproduced" caveat and note that its degree bound 9 is sharp.

**3.7 The affine D4 obstruction belongs in the main paper.** μ = 2 for x = product of the four leaves, w = xcx in affine D4 (recomputed) is what makes "finite simply laced" the natural scope and answers Green's "possibly all of them" negatively. The authors have it in `star-boundary.tex` but the main paper gives only the finite D4 example with μ = 0.

## 5. Significance assessment

The finite theorem's new content is an exhaustive terminal classification that is nontrivial only in E8, plus the observation that every new exceptional terminal has even gap to its unique eligible FC bottom, so unlike Gern's type D no KL computation beyond the inherited D6 value is needed. The reduction is Green's and Gern's; the lower-endpoint lemma is Fan's cell theorem as Green used it; the single nonzero coefficient is Gern's. In E6 the whole theorem is a two-second brute-force table, and E7 is within reach of Coxeter3.

That said, the result does answer two questions that were actually asked (Green 2009; Laird 2016 §5.3), it completes the finite simply laced case of Green's conjecture, and the exceptional terminal list is new structural information. The infinite families are modest root-theoretic constructions whose value is the structural corollary that terminals in E_n for n ≥ 9 cannot be listed finitely. With the all-k strengthening in 3.1, the E6 maximum-μ witness in 3.2, the affine odd-gap values in 3.3, and the Gern-plus-two statement in 3.4, the paper becomes noticeably sharper without new theorems.

Packaging: roughly 40% of the paper proves coefficient statements that are automatic from parity. An 8-page note centred on the finite classification, one infinite example, the affine D4 obstruction, and an explicit open-problem list (arbitrary upper endpoints in affine E8; classification of affine E8 terminals up to translation; the k ≥ 1 members of the length-33 family) would serve the result better. The two secondary notes say in their own abstracts that they establish no new theorem; fold their useful paragraphs into the main Scope section.

Venues: Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics or Algebraic Combinatorics are plausible but borderline at this depth; Experimental Mathematics, Journal of Algebra (computational section), Communications in Algebra, or Annals of Combinatorics are safer fits. Resolving the k ≥ 1 odd-gap cases or classifying affine E8 terminals would move it firmly into JACo territory.
