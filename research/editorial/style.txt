1. Overall assessment and priorities

The manuscript contains a clear finite-type theorem, a short structural vanishing argument, and a substantial uniform construction. The present order and voice obscure that hierarchy. Its organizing principle is the history of the investigation: E6/E7, verification, E8, fixed-rank families, uniform family, transfers, failed searches. A research article should instead organize these results by mathematical dependence and significance.

Highest-priority changes:
(a) Put the finite theorem and rank-uniform theorem in the introduction, immediately after definitions. State the uniform result there with quantifiers even if its explicit roots are deferred.
(b) Give the maximum-descent parity proposition a prominent explanatory paragraph. It is the conceptual reason that the explicit root constructions yield coefficient bounds without KL computations.
(c) Absorb E13 into the uniform family as its r=3 specialization. Keep E10 as a separate application. There is no reason to prove the E13 construction twice.
(d) Present E9 as the affine family in which the cover exception is also eliminated for every parameter; this distinguishes it sharply from the uniform eventual-vanishing conclusion.
(e) Consolidate computational methods and reproducibility information into a dedicated proof section and an appendix. Preserve mathematical completeness arguments in the article, but move long path lists and repeated assurance language out of the main narrative.
(f) Remove the investigation chronology, references to agents, state caps, unsuccessful search directions, and internal project vocabulary from the research article. Preserve them in the existing research reports.
(g) Consolidate priority uncertainty into one restrained statement. Repeating it in the abstract, introduction, structural section, uniform section, and final section diminishes rather than strengthens precision.

3. Proposed abstract (TeX-ready)

We study the leading coefficients $\mu(x,w)$ of ordinary equal-parameter
Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomials when $x$ is fully commutative. For finite
Weyl groups of types $E_6$, $E_7$, and $E_8$, we prove that
$\mu(x,w)\in\{0,1\}$ for every upper endpoint $w$. The proof combines
star reduction with exhaustive classifications of terminal elements;
parabolic pruning makes the $E_8$ classification possible without
full-group enumeration. We also derive a vanishing criterion from
maximum commuting descent sets and construct explicit full-support
terminal reflections in every generalized type $E_{4r+1}$, $r\geq3$.
For these families, all coefficients with fully commutative lower endpoint
vanish away from covers, and at each fixed rank they all vanish for
sufficiently large parameter. Further constructions give complete
vanishing for an infinite family in affine type $\widetilde E_8=E_9$
and the same non-cover vanishing in type $E_{10}$.

This abstract deliberately omits priority language, the conditional CI application, failed searches, and certificate file details. It calls the finite part computer-assisted by describing the method rather than using an apologetic final sentence. It does not claim a whole-type theorem in any infinite type, nor does it claim polynomial triviality. If desired, insert “computer-assisted” before “proof” in the second finite-method sentence.

4. Proposed introduction paragraphs (TeX-ready; place theorem statements between them)

For a Coxeter group $W$, the leading coefficients of the
Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomials record a distinguished part of the
Kazhdan--Lusztig basis multiplication. We consider the restriction
obtained by requiring the lower endpoint to be fully commutative.
Throughout, all Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomials are ordinary and
equal-parameter. Our first result establishes the $0/1$ bound in the
three finite exceptional simply laced types.

[Insert the finite theorem, with its current exact hypotheses. A theorem heading “Finite exceptional types” is cleaner than “Computer-assisted exceptional extension.” Explain the computational character directly after the theorem.]

The proof reduces arbitrary upper endpoints to terminal elements, meaning
those with no reduced expression beginning or ending in two
noncommuting generators. There are respectively one, four, and six
terminal elements in $E_6$, $E_7$, and $E_8$ that are not products of
commuting generators. For each such element, the descent conditions
leave a unique fully commutative lower endpoint to consider. Parity
settles all cases except a pair in a $D_6$ parabolic, whose coefficient
is determined by Gern's work. We prove completeness of the finite
classifications by exact computation; in type $E_8$, a parabolic
reduction avoids enumeration of the ambient group.

Gern proved the corresponding bound in type $D$
\cite[Theorem 4.5.11]{Gern}. Related results hold in finite and affine
type $A$ \cite{GreenLeading} and finite type $B$
\cite[\S2.3, Theorem 2.3.8]{Chmutov}. Green's result with both endpoints
fully commutative \cite[Remark 7.12]{GreenJones} and Jones's result
with Deodhar upper endpoint \cite[Theorem 1.2]{Jones} impose different
hypotheses. We do not claim priority for the finite exceptional result
or the explicit families below.

Our second result concerns the generalized diagrams $E_n$, whose arms
have lengths $1$, $2$, and $n-4$. For $n=9$ this is affine type
$\widetilde E_8$; for $n\geq10$ the group is indefinite. A structural
criterion supplies the coefficient vanishing: if the common left and
right descent set of an upper endpoint is a maximum independent set
in its support, then only its commuting product can satisfy the
necessary descent conditions on a fully commutative lower endpoint.
A parity condition then eliminates every non-cover coefficient.
We use this criterion to construct families in unbounded rank.

The roots defining these reflections are obtained from an explicit real
root by powers of an element in an affine parabolic subgroup. Real-root
membership of the initial vector is proved uniformly using an even
sign change in a finite $D_{4r}$ parabolic. Thus the construction and
coefficient argument require no Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomial computation.
We also give an affine $E_9$ family for which every coefficient with
fully commutative lower endpoint vanishes, and a separate family in
$E_{10}$ with non-cover vanishing.

7. Notation and typesetting

- Define mu by cases, or first say it is zero for even d and then give the coefficient formula only for odd d. The present [q^((d-1)/2)] formula formally uses a half-integer exponent before explaining the convention.
- Use E9 for the generalized rank-nine diagram and identify E9=tilde E8 once. Thereafter use one notation consistently in statements and headings; “affine E8” and “E9” currently alternate too freely.
- Spell out “fully commutative” in theorem statements, captions, and the abstract. FC is acceptable for the set notation and dense implementation discussion after definition.
- Prefer w_I=product_{s in I}s to i(I), if a global change is easy. It is recognizable Coxeter notation and makes commuting products easier to parse. No mathematical change is needed.
- Use supp(w), defined once, for repeated support hypotheses. State “a maximum independent set of the subgraph induced by supp(b)” rather than “maximum independent in the support.” Distinguish maximum cardinality from inclusion-maximal throughout.
- Replace manual equation tags (*), (**), (***) with \label and \eqref. Global star tags look provisional and make cross-references fragile.
- Number all tables and supply descriptive captions. Group terminal words and descent data so the reader need not alternate between prose and tables.
- The long digit strings are acceptable only in ranks <=10, where generator labels are single digits. State that convention locally. For general ranks use indexed generators or lists with separators.
- “Noncommuting terminal” is shorthand for a terminal element that is not a product of commuting generators, not a claim about an element itself commuting. Define the shorthand clearly or use “nontrivial terminal cases” only when the commuting-product exclusion is explicit nearby.
- Reserve “certificate” for a finite data object that a specified verifier checks. Use “proof” for the uniform even-sign-change argument. Avoid rhetorical uses such as “real-root certificate” when a two-line mathematical proof is present.
- Give the reflection notation r_beta and the root sign convention before first use. Separate group rank n from family parameter r and affine parameter k visually in statements.
- Remove date-specific adjectives such as “new invariance input.” Use “A conditional consequence of combinatorial invariance,” with the precise source and conditional hypothesis.
