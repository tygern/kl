CANDIDATE STANDALONE PROOF REWRITES (EXTRACTED FROM EXISTING ARGUMENTS)

A. Pruning lemma, shorter reduced-word proof.

Let w=av be the parabolic decomposition, with v in W_J and
ell(w)=ell(a)+ell(v). If v had a reduced expression ending in adjacent
generators ts, concatenating it with a reduced expression for a would give
such an expression for w. Thus a right-terminal w has right-terminal v.
The uniqueness of the parabolic decomposition now shows that testing all
av, with a in W^J and v right-terminal in W_J, finds every right-terminal
element of W. Applying the same test to the inverse selects the two-sided
terminals. [Then give the root-sum test for implementation separately.]

B. Maximum-common-descents lemma, expanded only where readers need a bridge.

Let K contain supp(x), let I be maximum independent in K, and suppose
I is contained in L(x) and R(x). The parabolic decomposition for W_I
shows that i(I) occurs as both a reduced prefix and a reduced suffix of x.
In Green's monomial Temperley--Lieb notation, a_TL(x) is the largest size
of a commuting product occurring as a length-additive factor. Hence
|I|<=a_TL(x)<=alpha(K)=|I|. Green's Proposition 4.4(iv), applied to the
prefix and suffix, places x in the right and left monomial cells of i(I).
By Proposition 4.4(v), their intersection consists of one element. Since
i(I) belongs to that intersection, x=i(I). These are monomial TL cells;
no identification with ordinary KL cells or ordinary a-values is needed.

C. Ambient translation lemma.

Suppose gamma and gamma+delta are real roots with gamma^2=2,
delta^2=(gamma,delta)=0, and put T=r_gamma r_(gamma+delta).
For every ambient vector rho, direct reflection multiplication gives
T rho=rho+(rho,delta)gamma-[(rho,gamma)+(rho,delta)]delta.
Writing N=T-1, one has N delta=0 and N gamma=-2delta; therefore
N^2 rho=-2(rho,delta)delta and N^3=0. If
(beta,gamma)=-c and (beta,delta)=-a, then
T^k beta=beta-ak gamma+(ak^2+ck)delta
         =beta+k[(a+c)delta-a gamma]+binom(k,2)2a delta.
This is an identity on the entire root space: delta need not lie in its
radical. In the indefinite applications (delta,alpha_8)=-1.
[Use c=r for the uniform seed and c=3,a=1 for E10.]

D. Reflection sign criterion, replacing duplicate prose in E10/uniform.

Let beta be a positive integral real root whose simple-root coordinates are
all strictly positive, and set m_j=(alpha_j,beta). Suppose the positive
m_j occur precisely on an independent set I and m_s+m_t<=0 whenever
s in I is adjacent to t. Then b=r_beta has L(b)=R(b)=I and is terminal.
Indeed b(alpha_j)=alpha_j-m_j beta. If m_j<=0 this is positive; if m_j>0,
integrality gives m_j>=1 and every coordinate is nonpositive, so it is
negative. For s in I and t adjacent to s,
bs(alpha_t)=alpha_t+alpha_s-(m_s+m_t)beta is positive. Thus no neighbor
of a right descent becomes a right descent after its removal. This is
right-terminality, and b=b^{-1} gives the left statement.

If some m_j is nonzero, b also has full support: column j of b-1 has
a nonzero entry in every row. Every product omitting a generator s_i fixes
coordinate row i, so b belongs to no proper standard parabolic.

E. Ending of the uniform theorem proof, after the three construction lemmas.

The matching (0,1),(2,4r),(3,4),...,(4r-3,4r-2) has 2r edges and leaves
one vertex uncovered. Every independent set therefore has at most 2r+1
vertices, and I_r attains this bound. Since b_(r,k) is a reflection,
ell(b_(r,k)) is odd, as is |I_r|. The maximum-descent parity criterion
gives the asserted bound and non-cover vanishing. If b_(r,k) were FC,
the maximum-common-descents lemma would give b_(r,k)=i(I_r). This is
impossible: their (-1)-eigenspaces have dimensions one and 2r+1.

Full support follows from the reflection sign lemma, since m_0=r-2>0.
The coordinate beta_(r,k),0=r-1+4k+(4r-8)k^2 strictly increases with k.
Distinct positive roots of norm two define distinct reflections, so the
family is injective. For fixed r, let M_r be the maximum length of an FC
element, which exists by FC-finiteness. Each bounded Coxeter-length ball
is finite; hence only finitely many b_(r,k) have length at most M_r+1.
For all remaining k no FC lower cover exists, and every other coefficient
has already been shown to vanish. No threshold uniform in r is asserted.
