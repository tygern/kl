EDITORIAL ASSESSMENT: exceptional-leading.tex

Scope: complete source read, including the affine matrix and inversion-count proof. This is an editorial architecture and claim-preservation review, not a fresh proof or priority audit. The main TeX was not edited.

CORE RECOMMENDATION

The manuscript has two principal contributions: the whole-type finite E6/E7/E8 theorem and the explicit rank-uniform E_(4r+1) family. Both deserve statements in the introduction. The maximum-descent parity criterion is the conceptual tool connecting the constructions and should be visible before the reader encounters detailed computation. The uniform theorem is currently the last discovery in the narrative and therefore reads as an addendum, although it is the strongest symbolic construction: all r >= 3, all k >= 0, full support at every rank, eventual complete FC-bottom vanishing, and a uniform real-root certificate inside finite D_(4r). Those features should be foregrounded.

Keep the finite theorem first: arbitrary upper endpoints in three entire groups is a different and broader quantifier than the selected infinite families. Give the uniform theorem equal prominence as the second main theorem, rather than presenting it as merely an extension of E13. Affine E8 and E10 are complementary constructions. The E13 construction is exactly the r=3 case and should cease to have a second full theorem/proof.

WHY THE CURRENT ORDER OBSCURES THE RESULTS

1. The opening says the essential new output is the finite classification, then the rank-uniform theorem appears many pages later. The abstract and introduction should reflect the final scope rather than the discovery sequence.
2. E6/E7 tables, implementation details, E8 pruning, and the E8 table occupy alternating mathematical/computational layers. The single finite theorem needs one complete terminal classification, one explanation of the sole D6 exception, and one computation-completeness argument.
3. The E13 proof is repeated in the uniform proof. The latter relies on an 'earlier operator identity' and on terminality 'exactly as in the fixed-rank proof', creating a dependence on the weaker result's placement. Extract the reusable identity once; make the uniform construction self-contained thereafter.
4. Repeated statements about unconfirmed priority, no whole-type infinite theorem, no full-polynomial claim, and no explicit cross-type interval isomorphisms disperse attention. State the relevant scope precisely in each theorem, give one concise comparison with prior work in the introduction, and keep one short concluding discussion. The detailed priority/search audit belongs in the archive.
5. Agent names, chronology, state caps, unsuccessful screens, sample indefinite lengths, and code paths read as a project log. They are not mathematical prerequisites for the positive theorems.

RECOMMENDED INTRODUCTION (PROSE DRAFT; INSERT FORMAL THEOREM STATEMENTS AT MARKERS)

We study leading coefficients of ordinary Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomials when the lower endpoint is fully commutative. For x<w, write mu(x,w) for the coefficient of q^((ell(w)-ell(x)-1)/2) in P_(x,w), with mu(x,w)=0 when the length difference is even; we also set mu(x,w)=0 for x not strictly below w. Our results concern both entire finite exceptional Weyl groups and explicit families of upper endpoints in infinite generalized E Coxeter groups.

The first result extends the fully commutative lower-endpoint bound to all three finite exceptional types.

[Insert existing finite theorem, thm:main, unchanged.]

The proof reduces arbitrary upper endpoints to terminal elements, those having no reduced expression that begins or ends with a noncommuting pair. We give exhaustive classifications of these elements in E6, E7 and E8. There are respectively one, four and six noncommuting terminals. In each case there is a unique fully commutative lower endpoint satisfying the necessary descent inclusions. Every resulting coefficient vanishes by parity except a pair in a D6 parabolic already handled by Gern. The E8 classification uses parabolic pruning and two independent decompositions, avoiding enumeration of the entire group.

Our second main result is an explicit construction that is uniform in both rank and a family parameter. Let E_n denote the simply laced diagram with arms of lengths 1, 2 and n-4. Thus E9 is affine E8, whereas E_n is indefinite for n>=10.

[Insert precise overview of thm:uniform: for every r>=3 the formulas in section 4 give distinct full-support non-FC terminal reflections b_(r,k), k>=0, in W(E_(4r+1)), with descent set I_r={0,3,5,...,4r-1,4r}; all FC-bottom coefficients belong to {0,1}, all non-cover coefficients vanish, and for each fixed r all FC-bottom coefficients vanish for sufficiently large k.]

The construction uses the entire diagram in every rank. Its proof is symbolic: an even sign change in a finite D_(4r) parabolic certifies the seed as a real root, and conjugation by an element of an embedded affine parabolic produces the family. A maximum-descent criterion then forces every potentially nonzero non-cover coefficient to have even length difference. In particular, the proof requires no Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomial computations.

Two complementary constructions cover ranks outside this uniform family. In affine E8 we give full-support terminal elements b_k of length 27+92k whose coefficients mu(x,b_k) vanish for every fully commutative x. In E10 we give infinitely many distinct full-support terminal reflections satisfying the 0/1 bound and non-cover vanishing. The E13 construction is the r=3 specialization of the rank-uniform theorem. These are families of upper endpoints; they do not establish a bound for arbitrary upper endpoints in an infinite E_n group.

The lower-endpoint condition is central. Gern proves the corresponding bound in type D; Green treats type A and affine type A; and Chmutov treats finite type B, equivalently the same Coxeter system in type C. Results with both endpoints fully commutative, such as Green's ADE statement, or with a Deodhar upper endpoint, as in Jones's theorem, impose different hypotheses. Our arguments use classical descent and star operations, Green's monomial Temperley--Lieb cell theory, and standard root and parabolic constructions. We make no claim of established publication priority for the exceptional classification or the explicit families; the detailed source audit is retained with the supplementary material.

We first establish the reduction and parity tools. We then prove the finite exceptional theorem and its exhaustive certification, construct the rank-uniform reflections, and give the complementary affine E8 and E10 families. The appendices record the exact finite certificates and reproduction procedures used in the computer-assisted parts of the proof.
