\begin{abstract}
We study the leading coefficients $\mu(x,w)$ of ordinary equal-parameter
Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomials when $x$ is fully commutative. For finite
Weyl groups of types $E_6$, $E_7$, and $E_8$, we prove
$\mu(x,w)\in\{0,1\}$ for every upper endpoint $w$. The proof combines
star reduction with exhaustive classifications of terminal elements;
parabolic pruning avoids full-group enumeration in type $E_8$.
Together with the known type-$A$ and type-$D$ results, this establishes
the bound for every finite simply laced Coxeter group.
We also construct full-support terminal reflections in every generalized
type $E_{4r+1}$, $r\geq3$. Green's monomial cell theory implies that their leading coefficients with fully
commutative lower endpoint vanish away from covers and, at each fixed
rank, vanish for all sufficiently large values of the family parameter.
An infinite family in affine type $\widetilde E_8=E_9$ has every such
leading coefficient equal to zero. A complementary $E_{10}$ construction
is recorded in an appendix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system, with length $\ell$ and Bruhat order $\leq$.
Write $P_{x,w}(q)$ for its ordinary equal-parameter Kazhdan--Lusztig
polynomials, and define
\[
 \mu(x,w)=\begin{cases}
 [q^{(\ell(w)-\ell(x)-1)/2}]P_{x,w}(q),
       &x<w\text{ and }\ell(w)-\ell(x)\text{ is odd},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
An element is fully commutative if any two of its reduced expressions
are related by interchanging adjacent commuting generators. Denote this set by $W_{\mathrm{FC}}$.
The first result concerns all upper endpoints in the finite exceptional groups.

\begin{theorem}[Finite exceptional types]\label{thm:main}
If $W$ has type $E_6$, $E_7$, or $E_8$, then
\[
 x\in W_{\mathrm{FC}},\quad w\in W
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mu(x,w)\in\{0,1\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
The proof extends Gern's type-$D$ star argument
\cite[Theorem 4.5.11]{Gern}. It reduces to a finite classification of
upper endpoints with no noncommuting initial or final pair in any reduced
expression. We call these elements \emph{terminal}; a terminal is
\emph{noncommuting} if it is not a product of mutually commuting generators.
There are respectively $1$, $4$, and $6$ noncommuting terminals in
$E_6$, $E_7$, and $E_8$. Each has a unique fully commutative lower endpoint
below it satisfying both descent inclusions. All resulting
coefficients vanish by parity except for a known pair in a $D_6$ parabolic.
The $E_8$ classification uses exhaustive parabolic pruning rather than
enumeration of its $696,729,600$ elements.

\begin{corollary}[Finite simply laced groups]\label{cor:finiteADE}
If $W$ is a finite simply laced Coxeter group, $x\in W_{\mathrm{FC}}$, and
$w\in W$, then $\mu(x,w)\in\{0,1\}$.
\end{corollary}
The irreducible type-$A$ and type-$D$ cases are due to Green
\cite{GreenLeading} and Gern \cite[Theorem 4.5.11]{Gern}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} supplies the exceptional cases; the direct-product
argument is given in Section~\ref{sec:finite}.

Our second result applies in arbitrarily large ranks. Throughout, the
generalized diagram $E_n$, $n\geq6$, is the chain $0-1-\cdots-(n-2)$ with node $n-1$
attached to node $2$. Thus $E_9=\widetilde E_8$, and $E_n$ is indefinite
for $n\geq10$. The numbering is
\[
 \begin{array}{ccccccc}
 &&&&n-1&&\\[-3pt]
 &&&&|&&\\[-3pt]
 0&-&1&-&2&-\cdots-&n-2
 \end{array}
\]
The \emph{support} of an element is the set of generators
occurring in its reduced expressions; full support means $\supp(w)=S$.

\begin{theorem}[A rank-uniform family]\label{thm:uniform}
For every integer $r\geq3$ there is an explicit family of distinct
full-support, non-fully-commutative terminal reflections
$b_{r,k}\in W(E_{4r+1})$, $k\geq0$.
For every $x\in W(E_{4r+1})_{\mathrm{FC}}$,
\[
 \mu(x,b_{r,k})\in\{0,1\},\qquad
 x<b_{r,k},\quad \ell(b_{r,k})-\ell(x)>1
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mu(x,b_{r,k})=0.
\]
For each fixed $r$, all these leading coefficients vanish for every
sufficiently large $k$.
\end{theorem}
These families show that, in the indicated infinite groups, the terminal
obstructions to length-decreasing star reduction cannot be exhausted by
a finite list. The construction in Section~\ref{sec:uniform} starts from a staircase
root and iterates an affine-parabolic root action. An even sign change in
$D_{4r}$ proves real-root membership uniformly in $r$. The common descent set is an independent set of maximum cardinality
and has odd size. Green's monomial
Temperley--Lieb cell theorem then restricts a potentially nonzero
non-cover coefficient to a single fully commutative lower endpoint,
and parity forces that coefficient to vanish. This proof requires no
Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomial computation.

Green proved the fully commutative lower-endpoint bound in type $A$ and
affine type $A$ \cite{GreenLeading}; Chmutov proved it in finite type $B$
\cite[\S2.3, Theorem 2.3.8]{Chmutov}, hence also for the identical
Coxeter system of type $C$. The two-FC-endpoint bound in generalized
type $E_n$ goes back to Graham's thesis, as recalled by Green
\cite[Remark 9.5]{GreenEn}. Green's trace formulas
\cite[Remark 7.12]{GreenJones} and Jones's result with Deodhar upper
endpoint \cite[Theorem 1.2]{Jones} have different endpoint hypotheses.
The structural input for both the finite lower-endpoint analysis and the
infinite families is Green's cell theory in generalized type $E$
\cite{GreenEn}. Our contribution is a complete certified exceptional
terminal classification and its coefficient consequence, together with
the explicit rank-uniform terminal construction. The descent and parity
mechanism is classical. We are not aware of an earlier proof of the
exceptional bound or an earlier description of these terminal lists and
rank-uniform families; historical priority remains unconfirmed.

Exceptional KL computations already feature in Geck's \emph{PyCox}
\cite{GeckPyCox} and Geck--Halls's type-$E_8$ cell analysis
\cite{GeckHalls}. Our assertion concerns every coefficient $\mu(x,w)$
with FC lower endpoint, rather than only cell partitions or leading
coefficients of character values. A direct appeal to FC cell closure is
unavailable in type $E$: Green--Losonczy's Example 2.2.5
\cite{GreenLosonczy} shows that this closure property fails there.
Moreover, a left $W$-graph action records a coefficient for a pair
$(x,w)$ only through a generator in $L(x)\setminus L(w)$
\cite[Remark 2.1]{GeckHalls}. Our residual terminal pairs have equal left
and right descent sets, so the cell action matrices alone do not determine
their coefficients.

The infinite-type results concern specified upper families, rather than
arbitrary upper endpoints. They control $\mu$, not all coefficients of
$P_{x,w}$. Section~\ref{sec:applications} gives the complementary affine
$E_8$ family; the rank-ten supplement is in Appendix~\ref{app:e10}. Section~\ref{sec:transfer} records a
conditional consequence of combinatorial invariance. Exact computational
certificates and reproduction instructions appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:certificates}.

\section{Structural reductions}\label{sec:reductions}
Write
\[
 L(w)=\{s\in S:\ell(sw)<\ell(w)\},\qquad
 R(w)=\{s\in S:\ell(ws)<\ell(w)\}.
\]
An independent vertex set in the Coxeter graph is a set of mutually
commuting simple generators. For such a set $I$, write
$i(I)=\prod_{s\in I}s$; the order of the factors is immaterial.
The terminal condition is Gern's ``weakly bad'' condition and Laird's
\emph{$T$-avoiding} condition \cite{Gern,Laird}; a terminal which is not a
commuting product is called ``bad'' in \cite{Gern}.
Laird listed the type-$E_n$ classification as a future direction
in 2016 \cite[\S5.3]{Laird}. We classify the finite exceptional cases and
give infinite families, without asserting a classification in infinite type.

\subsection{Star reduction}
We use the descent criterion and length-three star transport
\cite[\S2.3 and Theorem 4.2]{KL}, also stated in
\cite[Proposition 1.2.4]{Gern}. Full commutativity is preserved by these
stars in simply laced systems \cite[Proposition 2.10]{Shi}; see also
\cite[Proposition 1.1.29]{Gern}.
\begin{lemma}[Terminal reduction]\label{lem:reduction}
Let $W$ be simply laced. If $\mu(x,b)\in\{0,1\}$ for every terminal upper
endpoint $b$ and every fully commutative $x$, then the same conclusion
holds for all upper endpoints in $W$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on $\ell(w)$ and put $d=\ell(w)-\ell(x)$.
Noncomparable pairs give zero. For comparable pairs,
even length difference gives zero and difference one gives one.
Hence assume $x<w$ and $d\geq3$ is odd.
If $L(w)\not\subseteq L(x)$ or $R(w)\not\subseteq R(x)$, the descent
criterion gives zero: its only nonzero exception is a cover, already excluded.
We can thus assume both inclusions. Since the descent sets of a fully
commutative element are pairwise commuting, so are $L(w)$ and $R(w)$.

If $w$ is terminal, use the hypothesis. Otherwise, suppose first that
$w=stv$ is reduced with $m(s,t)=3$. Then $s\in L(w)$ and $t\notin L(w)$.
The left $\{s,t\}$-star is ${}^*w=sw=tv$, with
$\ell({}^*w)=\ell(w)-1$. Descent inclusion gives $s\in L(x)$, while full
commutativity gives $t\notin L(x)$. Therefore ${}^*x$ is defined and fully
commutative. Star transport gives
\[
 \mu(x,w)=\widetilde\mu({}^*x,{}^*w),
\]
where $\widetilde\mu$ is symmetric and zero on incomparable pairs.
The new length difference is $d$ or $d-2$, so
$\ell({}^*x)<\ell({}^*w)$.
If the new endpoints are incomparable the value is zero. Otherwise the
induction hypothesis applies to the shorter upper endpoint ${}^*w$.
The case of a noncommuting suffix uses right stars in the same way.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Maximum commuting descents}
The monomial cell structure is due to Fan \cite{Fan}; we use Green's
formulation \cite[\S4]{GreenEn}. The next lemma makes explicit the singleton-cell argument in the final
paragraph of Green's proof of Lemma 5.2 \cite{GreenEn}. It supplies the
lower-endpoint analysis in both the finite and infinite cases.
\begin{lemma}[Maximum common commuting descents]\label{lem:maxI}
Let $x$ be fully commutative in $E_n$, and let $K$ contain its support.
Let $I$ be an independent set of maximum cardinality in the induced graph
on $K$. If $I\subseteq L(x)\cap R(x)$, then $x=i(I)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use Green's \emph{monomial Temperley--Lieb} function $a_{\rm TL}$, the maximum
size of a commuting product occurring as a length-additive factor
\cite[Definition 4.1]{GreenEn}. The descent inclusions give both a prefix
and a suffix $i(I)$. Thus $a_{\rm TL}(x)\geq |I|$, whereas the support and
maximum-cardinality hypothesis give $a_{\rm TL}(x)\leq |I|$.
Green's Proposition 4.4(iv), and its left-hand version, put $x$ in both the
monomial right and left cells of $i(I)$. Proposition 4.4(v) says that this
intersection is a singleton, already containing $i(I)$. Therefore $x=i(I)$.
No identification of these monomial cells with ordinary KL cells is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Maximum-descent parity criterion]\label{prop:parity}
Let $b\in W(E_n)$ satisfy $L(b)=R(b)=I$, where $I$ is a maximum independent set
in the subgraph induced by $\supp(b)$. If $\ell(b)\equiv |I|\pmod2$, then for every FC $x$
\[
 \mu(x,b)\in\{0,1\},\qquad
 x<b,\ \ell(b)-\ell(x)>1\ \Longrightarrow\ \mu(x,b)=0.
\]
This criterion does not require $b$ to be terminal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a non-cover with a potentially nonzero coefficient, descent containment
is necessary. Since $x\leq b$, its support lies in that of $b$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:maxI} forces $x=i(I)$. The resulting length difference is
even, so its coefficient is zero. Covers have coefficient one.
\end{proof}

The independence number of $E_n$ is $\lceil n/2\rceil$. Excluding node $2$
leaves three paths with maximum total $2+\lceil(n-4)/2\rceil$;
including it gives $2+\lceil(n-5)/2\rceil$. Taking the larger proves the claim.

\subsection{Geometric terminal tests}
Normalize simple roots by $(\alpha_j,\alpha_j)=2$ and
$(\alpha_s,\alpha_t)=-1$ for adjacent nodes. For a real root $\beta$,
its reflection acts by $r_\beta(\rho)=\rho-(\rho,\beta)\beta$.
Root inequalities refer to positivity in the simple-root basis.

In these coordinates, the right-terminal test is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminaltest}
 s\in R(w)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 w(\alpha_s+\alpha_t)>0\quad\text{for every }t\sim s.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $(ws)(\alpha_t)=w(\alpha_s+\alpha_t)$ when $s\sim t$.
Testing $w^{-1}$ gives the left-terminal condition.

\subsection{Exhaustiveness of the classification}
All computations use the faithful integral geometric representation.
For columns $a_j=w(\alpha_j)$, right multiplication by $s_i$ replaces
\[
 a_i\longmapsto-a_i,\qquad a_j\longmapsto a_j+a_i\quad(j\sim i),
\]
and fixes the other columns. Signs determine descents, and
\eqref{eq:terminaltest} tests terminality on both sides. A noncommuting suffix
$ts$ is equivalent to $s\in R(w)$ and $t\in R(ws)$.
In $E_6$ and $E_7$, breadth-first multiplication by all generators until
closure enumerates the complete group and gives minimal word lengths.

For $E_8$, write the unique length-additive parabolic decomposition
$w=av$, with $a\in W^J$ and $v\in W_J$, where $W^J$ consists of the
minimal right coset representatives. Call an element \emph{right-terminal}
if it has no reduced expression ending in a noncommuting pair.

\begin{lemma}[Parabolic pruning]\label{lem:pruning}
If $w=av$ is right-terminal, then $v$ is right-terminal in $W_J$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A reduced expression for $v$ ending in adjacent generators would,
after prefixing a reduced expression for $a$, give such an expression
for $w$. Length additivity rules this out.
\end{proof}
Consequently one may enumerate all products $av$ with $v$ right-terminal,
apply \eqref{eq:terminaltest} in the ambient group, and then test inverses
to select the two-sided terminals. The recursive implementation follows
\[
 E_8\supset E_7\supset E_6\supset D_5\supset D_4\supset A_3
 \supset A_2\supset A_1\supset\{e\}.
\]
At each step all minimal coset representatives are obtained by closure
under left multiplication, reducing to right-$J$-minimal form by stripping
right $J$-descents. This reaches every coset because the left action on
right cosets is transitive. Starting with the identity, induction using
Lemma~\ref{lem:pruning} proves completeness at every stage.
The final $E_7$ parabolic has $576$ right-terminals and $240$ cosets.
Testing their products gives $2,160$ right-terminals in $E_8$; the inverse
test leaves exactly the $64$ terminals in
Proposition~\ref{prop:classification}.

The computation certifies the terminal classification; the eligible
lower endpoints follow analytically from Corollary~\ref{cor:eligible}.
In particular, the finite theorem requires neither an enumeration of all
FC--upper pairs nor a large KL polynomial table. Independent FC
catalogues provide a supplementary check of the lower-endpoint assertion.
Their closure test, counts, and certificate locations are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:certificates}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}, it suffices to treat terminal upper endpoints.
For a commuting product $b$, every $x<b$ is a proper subproduct. The
descent criterion gives zero on non-covers and one on covers.
For a noncommuting terminal, Table~\ref{tab:terminals} and parity leave
only the length-$15$ rows. These are the same pair in a $D_6$ parabolic.
In $E_n$, $n=7,8$, mapping Gern's labels $1,2,3,4,5,6$ to
$1,n-1,2,3,4,5$ identifies the
eligible pair with
\[
 x_6=(-1,-2,4,3,6,5),\qquad w_6=(-1,-6,3,-4,5,-2).
\]
Bruhat order and ordinary KL polynomials agree with those in the standard
parabolic. Gern's Lemma~4.5.5 \cite{Gern} gives $\mu(x_6,w_6)=1$.
All other non-cover coefficients with terminal upper endpoint are zero.
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} now proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:finiteADE}]
The irreducible classification is $A$, $D$, and $E$; combine Green's
type-$A$ theorem, Gern's type-$D$ theorem, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
For a direct product $W=\prod_j W_j$, full commutativity is componentwise
and $P_{x,w}=\prod_jP_{x_j,w_j}$. Suppose $x<w$, and let $m$ be the number
of components with positive length difference $d_j$ and $d=\sum_jd_j$.
An even $d$ gives $\mu=0$. For odd $d$ and $m\geq2$, the usual degree
bound gives
\[
 \deg P_{x,w}\leq\sum_{d_j>0}\frac{d_j-1}{2}
       =\frac{d-m}{2}<\frac{d-1}{2},
\]
so $\mu=0$. For $m=1$ it is the coefficient in the single nontrivial
component, which lies in $\{0,1\}$. Noncomparable pairs give zero.
\end{proof}

\section{The rank-uniform construction}\label{sec:uniform}
Fix an integer $r\geq3$ and put $n=4r+1$.

Number the chain
$0-1-\cdots-(4r-1)$ and attach node $4r$ to node $2$.
Define a vector $\beta_r$ by
\[
 (\beta_{r,0},\beta_{r,1},\beta_{r,2},\beta_{r,4r})
       =(r-1,r,2r-1,r),\qquad
 \beta_{r,j}=2r-\lfloor j/2\rfloor\quad(3\leq j\leq4r-1).
\]
Thus the long-arm coordinates are
$2r-1,2r-2,2r-2,\ldots,1,1$.
Put
\[
 I_r=\{0,3,5,\ldots,4r-1,4r\},\qquad a=2r-4.
\]
Use the embedded affine $E_8$ subdiagram on $\{0,\ldots,7,4r\}$ and set
\begin{align*}
 (\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_7)&=(2,4,6,5,4,3,2,1),&\delta_{4r}&=3,\\
 (\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_7)&=(1,2,3,2,2,1,1,0),&\gamma_{4r}&=1.
\end{align*}
All their other coordinates vanish. Set $T=r_\gamma r_{\gamma+\delta}$.
For $k\geq0$ define
\begin{equation}
 \beta_{r,k}=\beta_r-ak\gamma+(ak^2+rk)\delta,
 \qquad b_{r,k}=r_{\beta_{r,k}}.                     \label{eq:uniformroots}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\subsection{The staircase seed}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:uniformseed}
For every $r\geq3$, the vector $\beta_r$ is a real root of $E_{4r+1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}

Deleting node $0$ gives finite type $D_{4r}$. In an
orthonormal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_{4r}$ realize its simple roots as
\[
 \alpha_1=e_1+e_2,\qquad\alpha_{4r}=e_2-e_1,\qquad
 \alpha_j=e_{j+1}-e_j\quad(2\leq j\leq4r-1).
\]
Take $z$ orthogonal to this space, with $(z,z)=2-r$, and put
$\alpha_0=z-\frac12\sum_i e_i$. These vectors have exactly the
generalized $E_{4r+1}$ Cartan inner products. Expanding the displayed seed
gives
\[
 \beta_r=(r-1)z+\sum_{i=1}^{4r}c_i e_i,\qquad
 c_i=\begin{cases}-(r-1)/2&i\text{ odd},\\(3-r)/2&i\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Since $(\beta_r,\alpha_0)=r-2$, we have
\[
 s_0\beta_r=z-\tfrac12\sum_{i\text{ odd}}e_i
                       +\tfrac12\sum_{i\text{ even}}e_i.
\]
This equals $F\alpha_0$, where $F$ flips the $2r$ even coordinates.
An even sign change belongs to $W(D_{4r})$
\cite[Example 1.1.6]{Gern}. Explicitly, it is a product
of $r$ double sign changes, each given by
$r_{e_{4j-2}-e_{4j}}r_{e_{4j-2}+e_{4j}}$, $1\leq j\leq r$.
Therefore $\beta_r=s_0F\alpha_0$ is a real root for every $r$.

\end{proof}

\subsection{The affine-parabolic action}
We record the elementary reflection calculation underlying the iteration.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rootaction}
Suppose that $\gamma$ and $\gamma+\delta$ are real roots, with
$(\gamma,\gamma)=2$ and $(\gamma,\delta)=(\delta,\delta)=0$.
For $T=r_\gamma r_{\gamma+\delta}$,
\[
 (T-1)\rho=(\rho,\delta)\gamma-
       ((\rho,\gamma)+(\rho,\delta))\delta,\qquad (T-1)^3=0.
\]
If $(\beta,\delta)=-a$ and $(\beta,\gamma)=-c$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rootiterate}
 T^k\beta=\beta-ak\gamma+(ak^2+ck)\delta\qquad(k\geq0).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first identity follows by multiplying the two reflection formulas.
Set $N=T-1$. Then $N\delta=0$ and $N\gamma=-2\delta$, so $N^3=0$.
Moreover $N\beta=-a\gamma+(a+c)\delta$ and $N^2\beta=2a\delta$.
The binomial expansion of $(1+N)^k$ gives \eqref{eq:rootiterate}.
\end{proof}
The lemma applies on the whole ambient root space. In the indefinite
constructions below, $\delta$ is null in an affine parabolic but is not
in the radical of the ambient form.

\subsection{Translated roots and descent sets}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:uniformdescents}
For all $r\geq3$ and $k\geq0$, $\beta_{r,k}=T^k\beta_r$ is a real root
with every coordinate strictly positive. Its reflection is terminal and
has both descent sets equal to $I_r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The vector $\gamma$ is a root of the finite $E_8$ parabolic obtained by
omitting node $7$, and $\gamma+\delta$ is an affine real root.
Fixed lowering witnesses are given in Appendix~\ref{app:affinedata}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rootaction} applies to $T$. Direct pairing with the seed gives
\[
 (\beta_r,\gamma)=-r,\qquad(\beta_r,\delta)=-a.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:rootiterate} gives
$T^k\beta_r=\beta_{r,k}$. Thus every vector in \eqref{eq:uniformroots} is a real root.
In the binomial form its first increment is
$(a+r)\delta-a\gamma$ and its second increment is $2a\delta$.
Both are coordinatewise nonnegative for $r\geq3$, so every coordinate
of $\beta_{r,k}$ is strictly positive.

Set $m_j=(\alpha_j,\beta_{r,k})$. The pairings are
\begin{align*}
 m_0&=r-2,&m_1&=2-r,&m_2&=-1-ak,\\
 m_j&=(-1)^{j+1}(1+ak)&&(3\leq j\leq7),\\
 m_8&=-1-ak^2-rk,\\
 m_j&=(-1)^{j+1}&&(9\leq j\leq4r-1),&m_{4r}&=1+ak.
\end{align*}
They are positive exactly on $I_r$. The formula
\[
 b_{r,k}(\alpha_j)=\alpha_j-m_j\beta_{r,k}
\]
is negative exactly when $m_j>0$, since $\beta_{r,k}$ is a strictly
positive integral real root. Hence $R(b_{r,k})=I_r$, and involutivity
gives $L(b_{r,k})=I_r$. For every edge incident to $I_r$,
the sum of its two pairings is nonpositive. At the exceptional edge
$7-8$ this sum is $-ak(k-1)-rk\leq0$ for integer $k\geq0$; at $8-9$
it is $-ak^2-rk$. The remaining edge sums are nonpositive by the displayed formulas.
For $s\in I_r$ and $t\sim s$,
\[
 b_{r,k}(\alpha_s+\alpha_t)
 =\alpha_s+\alpha_t-(m_s+m_t)\beta_{r,k}>0.
\]
Thus deleting a right descent creates no adjacent right descent.
The reflection is an involution, so the same holds on the left.

\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}]
The set $I_r$ is independent of size $2r+1$.
The matching
\[
 (0,1),(2,4r),(3,4),(5,6),\ldots,(4r-3,4r-2)
\]
has $2r$ edges and leaves one vertex unmatched, proving that $I_r$ is maximum.
Every reflection has odd length, so Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}
proves the coefficient assertions.
If $b_{r,k}$ were FC, Lemma~\ref{lem:maxI} would
force it to be $i(I_r)$, whose $(-1)$-eigenspace has dimension $2r+1$,
whereas a real-root reflection has dimension one.

Full support follows because $(\alpha_0,\beta_{r,k})=r-2>0$ and every root coordinate is
positive: column $0$ of the reflection changes every coordinate row.
Each simple reflection changes only its own coordinate of a vector, so
an omitted generator would leave that coordinate row unchanged. Distinctness
follows from
\[
 \beta_{r,k,0}=r-1+4k+(4r-8)k^2.
\]
Distinct positive norm-two roots give distinct reflections, since
the image of $1-r_\beta$ is the line spanned by $\beta$. Finally,
the FC set in each fixed generalized $E_n$ is finite
\cite[Theorem 5.1]{Stembridge}. Finite Coxeter balls and distinctness
imply $\ell(b_{r,k})\to\infty$ as $k\to\infty$. For sufficiently large $k$, its length exceeds the maximum FC length
by more than one, so cover coefficients are also excluded.
\end{proof}

At $r=3$ the seed is
\[
 \beta_3=(2,3,5,5,4,4,3,3,2,2,1,1,3),
\]
giving the $E_{13}$ family. The restriction $r\geq3$ is structural:
at $r=2$, $(\alpha_0,\beta_2)=0$ and the maximum odd descent set is lost.
The argument does not give an effective threshold for the eventual
vanishing in Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}.

\section{An affine vanishing family}\label{sec:applications}

Use $E_9$ numbering: chain $0-1-\cdots-7$, with node $8$ attached to $2$.
The affine node is $7$. Coordinates are in the simple-root basis, with
adjacent pairings $-1$ and squared simple-root lengths $2$. Put
\begin{align*}
 \delta&=(2,4,6,5,4,3,2,1,3),\\
 \gamma&=(1,1,1,1,1,0,0,0,0),& I&=\{1,3,5,7,8\},\\
 b_0&=312875645234123012856745231.
\end{align*}
Let $T=r_\gamma r_{\gamma+\delta}$, so that
$T(\alpha)=\alpha-(\alpha,\gamma)\delta$, and define
$b_k=T^k b_0T^{-k}$ for integers $k\geq0$.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit affine family]\label{thm:affine}
Every $b_k$ is a full-support terminal element in affine type
$\widetilde E_8$ that is not fully commutative, with
\[
 L(b_k)=R(b_k)=I,\qquad \ell(b_k)=27+92k.
\]
For every fully commutative $x\in W(E_9)$, one has $\mu(x,b_k)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The vector $\delta$ is the null root, and $\gamma$ is a root of the finite
$E_8$ parabolic obtained by omitting node $7$. Multiplying the displayed word gives $b_0^2=1$.
Write $B_0$ for the matrix with columns $b_0(\alpha_j)$.
The matrix is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:affinedata}. Its columns
are negative precisely at the indices in $I$, and for every edge incident
to $I$ the sum of the corresponding columns is a positive root. These checks prove that $b_0$ is terminal on both sides.
Put
\[
 d=(2,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,-1,-1).
\]
Computing $(\alpha_j,\gamma-b_0\gamma)$ gives $d_j$, and conjugation yields
\begin{equation}
 b_k(\alpha_j)=b_0(\alpha_j)+k d_j\delta.                 \label{eq:affinecolumns}
\end{equation}
The base columns are negative on $I$ and positive off $I$; $d$ has the
same sign pattern. Thus the descent set stays $I$. The involution property
persists under conjugation, giving the same left descent set.
On every edge $s\sim t$ with $s\in I$, one has $d_s+d_t\geq0$.
Consequently \eqref{eq:affinecolumns} preserves positivity of $b_k(\alpha_s+\alpha_t)$, which
is precisely the right-terminal test. Involution gives the left test.
At $k=0$ the displayed reduced word has full support. At $k\geq1$ each
descent column is strictly negative in every coordinate. Omitting a
simple generator would leave its coordinate row unchanged, contradicting
these columns. Distinctness follows from the nonzero column slopes.

For completeness, count affine inversions to obtain the exact length.
For each of the $240$ finite roots $\eta$, write
$b_0\eta=\eta'+h\delta$ and let $\epsilon(\eta)$ be $0$ for positive finite
roots and $1$ for negative ones. The positive affine roots with finite
part $\eta$ are $\eta+n\delta$, $n\geq\epsilon(\eta)$.
Their contribution to the length of $b_k$ is
\[
 \max(0,A_\eta+kB_\eta),\quad
 A_\eta=\epsilon(\eta')-h-\epsilon(\eta),\quad
 B_\eta=-\sum_j\eta_j d_j.
\]
Finite-root enumeration groups the potentially nonzero contributions
by $(A_\eta,B_\eta)$ as follows:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$(A,B)$ & $(0,1)$ & $(0,2)$ & $(1,0)$ & $(1,1)$ & $(1,2)$\\
Multiplicity & 44 & 8 & 1 & 20 & 6\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The remaining $161$ contributions are identically zero for $k\geq0$.
The sum is $27+92k$. Appendix~\ref{app:certificates} identifies the complete root-string certificate.

The set $I$ is a maximum independent set of size $5$. Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}
therefore gives zero on all FC non-covers. The complete FC catalogue has maximum length $44$, so there are no FC covers when $k\geq1$.
For $k=0$, delete one letter from the reduced word and retain products
of length $26$. This gives all $21$ distinct Bruhat covers, none FC.
It eliminates the cover exception. If $b_k$ were FC,
Lemma~\ref{lem:maxI} would force $b_k=i(I)$, contradicting
$\ell(b_k)=27+92k>5$.
\end{proof}

The first indefinite rank $E_{10}$ is outside the uniform sequence
$E_{4r+1}$, $r\geq3$. Appendix~\ref{app:e10} gives a complementary
full-support terminal reflection family in that rank, with maximum
common descent set $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ and the same non-cover vanishing
conclusion (Proposition~\ref{thm:indefinite}). Standard-parabolic embeddings
transport the affine and rank-ten examples to larger $E_n$ groups;
these embedded families retain the support of their original parabolics.

\section{Scope}\label{sec:transfer}
\paragraph{Interval transfer.}
Assuming the combinatorial-invariance theorem of \cite[Theorem 1.1]{CI},
an interval isomorphic to a finite simply laced FC-bottom interval has
$\mu\in\{0,1\}$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:finiteADE}. The same observation
transfers the uniform and fixed-rank bounds to intervals admitting those
models, with $\mu=0$ for an affine-family model. Indeed, the polynomial
and relative rank are preserved. The target bottom need not be FC.
This is a formal conditional consequence; no new family of cross-type
interval isomorphisms is asserted.

\paragraph{Full polynomials.}
Vanishing of $\mu$ does not imply $P=1$. In $D_4$, let $x$ be the product
of the three leaves and let $c$ be the center. The element $b=xcx$ has
maximum common commuting descents and an even gap $\ell(b)-\ell(x)=4$,
but $P_{x,b}=1+2q$ \cite[\S3, preprint Corollary 3]{Mongelli}.
Thus the distinction between the leading coefficient and the full
polynomial is essential, even for the descent mechanism used here.

\section{An indefinite \texorpdfstring{$E_{10}$}{E10} supplement}\label{app:e10}
Embed the affine subdiagram on $\{0,\ldots,7,9\}$ and take
\[
 \delta=(2,4,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,3),\qquad
 \gamma=(1,2,3,2,2,1,1,0,0,1).
\]
Here $\gamma$ differs from the root used in the affine family of
Section~\ref{sec:applications}.
Set
\[
 \beta_0=(3,7,10,9,7,6,4,3,1,6),\qquad I=\{1,3,5,7,9\},
\]
set $T=r_\gamma r_{\gamma+\delta}$ and, for integers $k\geq0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:e10roots}
 \beta_k=\beta_0-k\gamma+(k^2+3k)\delta,\qquad b_k=r_{\beta_k}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}\label{thm:indefinite}
For integers $k\geq0$, the reflections in \eqref{eq:e10roots} are
distinct full-support terminal elements of $W(E_{10})$, with
$L(b_k)=R(b_k)=I$. For every fully commutative $x$,
$\mu(x,b_k)\in\{0,1\}$, and $\mu(x,b_k)=0$ whenever
$x<b_k$ and $\ell(b_k)-\ell(x)>1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lowering word in Appendix~\ref{app:affinedata} reduces $\beta_0$
to $\alpha_9$, proving real-root membership. The roots $\gamma$ and
$\gamma+\delta$ are real roots of the embedded affine parabolic, with
$(\beta_0,\gamma)=-3$ and $(\beta_0,\delta)=-1$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rootaction} gives $\beta_k=T^k\beta_0$.
The binomial increments $4\delta-\gamma$ and $2\delta$ are nonnegative,
so every coordinate of $\beta_k$ is strictly positive.
Its simple-root pairings are
\begin{align*}
 ((\alpha_j,\beta_k))_{j=0}^9
 ={}&(-1,1,-2-k,1+k,-1-k,1+k,-1-k,1+k,\\
    &\hspace{30mm}-1-k^2-3k,2+k).
\end{align*}
The positive entries occur exactly on $I$, and their sums with adjacent
entries are nonpositive. The reflection formula and
\eqref{eq:terminaltest} give both descent sets and terminality.
The constant pairing $(\alpha_1,\beta_k)=1$ makes column $1$ change
every coordinate row, proving full support. The coordinate
$\beta_{k,0}=2k^2+5k+3$ proves distinctness.
The set $I$ is a maximum independent set of size $5$. Reflection parity and
Proposition~\ref{prop:parity} give the coefficient assertions.
As in Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}, the reflections are not FC because their
$(-1)$-eigenspaces have dimension one, whereas that of $i(I)$ has dimension five.
\end{proof}
The base reflection has length $101$. No formula for the lengths of the
indefinite families is needed. Standard-parabolic embeddings transport
the $E_9$ and $E_{10}$ families to larger generalized $E_n$ groups;
their supports remain those of the smaller parabolics.
